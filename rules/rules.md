# Driven - Street Level Heroic RPG

Rules version 2026-11-17.32 · Nov 17, 2026 · @Stephen

## Overview

Driven is a tactical street-level heroic RPG about vigilantes who get worn down by the city, and whose flaws break before their bodies do. Fights are quick and played on a simple zone map. Everything around the fights (cases, civilian life, the cops, the press) runs on clocks and Traits.

**Core idea:** your Trait is your strength and your weak spot. Pressure (Stress) makes it stronger, then makes it dangerous, then breaks you.

**What you need:** 2d10 per player (a third d10 for Advantage), a GM, character sheets, and a sheet of paper for the zone map. Minis or tokens are optional.

**Scale:** heroes are street level. Top stat is +4. Guns hurt. Nobody throws buses.

**How a session runs:** each session is one **Night**. Heroes investigate (Patrol), fight (Confrontation), then deal with the fallout in their civilian lives (Dawn). A case usually takes 2 to 4 Nights. An arc of several cases ends with a chance to change your Trait (Old Habits).

## The core roll

Every roll is **2d10 + a stat against a defense**. The amount you beat it by sets your **Impact**.

**Margin** = your total minus the defense.

| Margin | Result | In a fight | Outside a fight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Below 0 | Miss | The GM makes a move against you | It fails or goes wrong, GM makes a move |
| 0 to 3 | Impact 1 | 1 Harm, or the attack's effect. No cost | You get it, with a cost: the GM offers two, you pick one |
| 4 to 7 | Impact 2 | 2 Harm | Clean success |
| 8+ | Impact 3 | 3 Harm | Success plus a bonus (a clue, a position, an opening) |

The defense is always **12 + a stat**, either the target's or an obstacle's rating (see Stats and Defenses). Every roll in the game uses this one table.

**Impact 1.** An attack never carries a cost: an attack at Impact 1 deals 1 Harm or its effect. Any other roll at Impact 1, in or out of a fight, succeeds with a cost: the GM offers two that fit the scene and you pick one (see GM moves, Costs).

**Impact tops out at 3.** Bonuses from weapons, powers and Signatures add **Harm**, not Impact, so a hit can deal 4 or more Harm, but the roll's Impact stays what the dice made it. An Impact 2 with a crowbar deals 3 Harm and is still an Impact 2: it drops one mook, not two. Anything that triggers on Impact 3 (a second mook dropping, the Impact 3 bonus) needs a real Impact 3 from the roll, or a Drive upgrade.

**Edge:** +2 to the roll. Only one Edge per roll, whatever the source.

**Advantage / Disadvantage:** roll 3d10, keep the best two (or the worst two). They cancel each other out and never stack: if a roll has any Advantage and any Disadvantage, it has neither, no matter how many sources of each. Edge and Advantage can apply to the same roll.

**Odds at a glance** (your stat vs the stat behind the defense):

| Your stat vs theirs | Miss | Impact 1 | Impact 2 | Impact 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 lower | 79% | 18% | 3% | 0% |
| Equal | 55% | 30% | 14% | 1% |
| 2 higher | 36% | 36% | 22% | 6% |
| 3 higher | 28% | 36% | 26% | 10% |
| 5 higher | 15% | 30% | 34% | 21% |

Edge is worth about the same as being 2 points better. Advantage is a little stronger, about 2.5 points.

## Stats and defenses

Every stat is used both to act and to defend, so no build is left wide open.

| Stat | Act with it to | Defense | Defends against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Force | Hit hard, break things, lift, hold on | **Toughness** (see below) | Reduces the Harm you take |
| Flow | Move, dodge, sneak, shoot, improvise | **Guard** = 12 + Flow | Punches, bullets, thrown things |
| Focus | Investigate, hack, use gadgets, read people | **Wits** = 12 + Focus | Ambushes, tricks, illusions, lies |
| Presence | Intimidate, inspire, persuade, taunt | **Poise** = 12 + Presence | Fear, threats, manipulation |

**Toughness:** Force +3 or higher gives Toughness 1. Toughness reduces the Harm of each hit by 1, after weapon bonuses, but a hit always does at least 1 Harm: an Impact 1 hit against Toughness 1 still deals 1 Harm. The roll's Impact is unchanged. It doesn't work against guns unless you have armor or a power that says it does. Toughness doesn't stack: use your single highest source for that attack. Only effects that say "+1 Toughness" (Stand Your Ground, Hold the line) add on top. Toughness can never go above 2. Toughness covers physical blows, blades, and (with armor) bullets. Gas, fire, electricity, and other hazards ignore it unless a power or piece of armor says otherwise.

| Where your Toughness comes from | Blunt | Blades | Guns | Electricity, gas, fire |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Force +3 or higher | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| Kevlar vest | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| Tactical armor, Armored power | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| Powered armor | 2 | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| Resilience (street power) | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Take the single highest row that applies to the attack, never two added together. A hero with Force +4 and a Kevlar vest has Toughness 1 against a punch and Toughness 1 against a bullet, never 2.

**Presence attacks deal Stress, not Harm.** A villain who rattles you with a threat rolls Presence vs your Poise.

### Starting stats

Pick one array and assign it to your four stats:

- **Specialist:** +4, +2, +1, -1
- **Balanced:** +3, +2, +1, 0

Stats range from -1 to +4. Nothing in the game goes higher than +4 for heroes.

### Obstacles

Things that aren't people get a rating, and the defense is 12 + rating. Roll whichever stat fits how you approach it.

| Rating | Defense | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| -1 | 11 | A locked door, a chain-link fence, a nervous witness |
| +1 | 13 | A police database, a three-story drop, a crowd turning ugly |
| +3 | 15 | A corporate server, a car going 60, a hardened mob boss |

If there's no real opposition and failure wouldn't be interesting, don't roll. Just say it happens.

## Harm

Harm is damage to your body. Each point of Impact fills one Harm box, top to bottom.

### Heroes: 4 boxes

| Box | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Bruised | No effect. Clears at the end of every scene (see Recovery). |
| Hurt | No effect yet, but you're one box from real trouble. |
| Wounded | Disadvantage on Force and Flow rolls. |
| Down | You're out of the fight. You can talk, crawl, or make one desperate Quick Action per round at Disadvantage. Or spend your whole turn on Second Wind (3 Drive, no roll, once per fight). |

**Past Down:** if a Downed hero takes more Harm, they're **Dying**. A single hit stops at Down, however big it is: only a later hit makes you Dying. An ally can spend a Full Action next to a Dying hero to stabilize them.

**Death is decided at the table (trial).** Comic heroes rarely die and stay dead, and no hero here dies to a dice roll. If a Dying hero hasn't been stabilized by the end of the scene, their player decides. They can say this is where the hero's story ends. Otherwise the hero is **left for dead**: they survive, stay Down until Dawn like any stabilized hero, and tick their Home clock twice, because you're too fucked up to manage your life at all. A power that works "when you would die" works when you would be left for dead, and spares you that cost. Going after a Downed hero is still the hardest move the GM has: use it when the villain is a killer or the fiction demands it, and say it out loud first ("He's going to finish you") so the team can act.

**Enemies too.** An enemy who drops is out of the fight, not dead. Whether anyone actually dies is the table's call: the hero who dropped them says what they meant to do, and the table agrees what that means for the story.

**Grit your teeth (trial).** At the start of your turn, a Wounded hero can take 1 Stress to ignore the Wounded Disadvantage until their next turn.

### Mooks: 1 box

Any hit takes a mook out. An Impact 3 hit also drops a second mook in the same zone. When mooks attack, they deal at most **2 Harm** per attack. Apply the cap last, after the gun bonus, armor, and Toughness.

**Bruisers (trial).** Some mooks are tougher: enforcers, bouncers, riot cops. A bruiser has 2 Harm boxes and no Toughness. Bruisers take Harm the way a named enemy does, so weapon bonuses count. Every 2 Harm drops one, and Harm left over carries to the next bruiser in the group. An Impact 3 doesn't drop a second bruiser by itself: its Harm drops one and staggers the next. Anything that takes a mook out with no Harm (a tranquilizer dart, a taser) deals 1 Harm to a bruiser instead. In every other way they are mooks: they act as a group, their attacks cap at 2 Harm, and they lose turns as written.

### Named villains: 5 to 10 boxes

Lieutenants have 5. A case's main villain has 10, or 8 for a smaller one, and 2 more when they fight alone (trial). Villains don't take penalties as they fill up, but many have a Trait that kicks in when they're Fraying (see Villains).

### Weapons

Everyone carries a knife and one free weapon. The table below is the short version. Weapons you carry (under Gear and Powers) has the full list, with the bow and the shotgun.

| Weapon | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Fists, kicks, thrown junk | No change |
| Light melee (baton, fighting sticks, weighted gloves) | +1 Harm on a Strike at Impact 2 or better. Jabs with it have no -2 penalty |
| Heavy melee (bat, pipe, crowbar) | +1 Harm on any hit, but only on a Strike with Force (never a Jab, never Flow) |
| Blades | +1 Harm on Impact 2 or better against anyone with no Toughness |
| Guns | +1 Harm on Impact 2 or better (a graze is just a graze) and ignores natural Toughness, though armor still works. Firing one is loud: +1 Heat for each fight where you fire one |

**Armor** (a vest, a reinforced suit) gives Toughness as listed under Gear and Powers. A hero with Force +3 and armor still has Toughness 1, not 2.

### Recovery

- **Breather:** Bruised clears automatically at the end of every scene, unless the GM says there is no time. It clears only the Bruised box. Hurt and Wounded stay filled, so a Wounded hero stays Wounded, and the next Harm fills Bruised again.
- **Dawn** (see The Night): Patch Up clears Hurt and Wounded.
- **Down** after a fight: when the fight ends, a hero who is Down (but not Dying) gets back on their feet at Wounded and can carry on with the Night, at Disadvantage on Force and Flow until they heal. A hero who was Dying and got stabilized stays Down until Dawn, and only clears it with Patch Up and Sleep It Off together. Second Wind and a med kit get you up at Wounded in the middle of a fight.
- **Dying leaves a mark (trial).** A hero who was Dying and got stabilized starts the next Night with one Harm box filled that Patch Up at that Dawn can't clear. A med kit, Fast Healer, Healing Factor, a Lair or a back-alley clinic can clear it. It doesn't stack with left for dead.

## Stress and Traits

Harm is your body. Stress is your nerves, your temper, your life falling apart. Your **Trait** decides what Stress does to you: it makes you stronger, then dangerous, then it breaks you.

### The Stress track: 8 boxes

| Stress | Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 3 | **Steady** | Your Trait's Steady perk is on. |
| 4 to 7 | **Fraying** | Your Trait's Fraying perk replaces the Steady perk. The GM gets your Trait's hook. |
| 8 | **Breaking** | Your Trait's Breaking fallout takes over for the rest of the scene. |

**You gain Stress when:**

- you Strain for an extra action (1)
- a Presence attack hits you (1 Stress per Impact)
- someone you're protecting gets hurt or dies on your watch (1 to 2)
- the GM makes a hard move against you (1, on top of what the move costs)
- you act against your Trait (1)

**How Stress builds** (it should climb steadily across a Night):

1. **Scheme ticks wear everyone down.** Each time the Scheme clock ticks, someone takes 1 Stress. If a hero's roll made it tick (a Miss or a cost on a lead), only that hero takes it. If it ticks at Dawn or from a missed Crack the Case, every hero takes 1.
2. **Going Down hurts more than your body.** A hero who goes Down takes 2 Stress. Every hero who sees an ally go Down takes 1, but only the first time in each fight. **Down and Stress (trial):** after that, a Downed hero takes no Stress from outside sources until they're back on their feet, including the Dawn Scheme tick. Stress the hero chooses to pay (Strain, Push, power costs) is still paid.
3. **Villains get in your head.** Every named villain has a Presence attack in their stat block (vs Poise, deals Stress). Once per round they can use it as a free extra action, on top of their normal turn, so threats never cost them an attack.
4. **Stress as a cost.** When a roll that isn't an attack lands at Impact 1, the GM can make 1 Stress one of the two costs on offer, when it fits the fiction. At 8 Stress, the GM leaves Stress off and offers two other costs.

With these in play, Sleep It Off at Dawn becomes a real choice.

**After Breaking:** at the end of the scene, drop to 4 Stress, gain 2 Drive, and tick your Home clock once (it follows you home).

**Clearing Stress:** mostly at Dawn (see The Night). Stress carries over from Night to Night if you don't deal with it.

**The GM's hook:** while you're Fraying, the GM can use your hook to push a complication on you. If you go along with it, gain 1 Drive. If you refuse, take 1 Stress.

### Traits

Pick one. Each Trait lists how it earns Drive, its two perks, its hook, and how you break.

**Your own Trait (trial).** With the GM's agreement you can write your own instead. It needs all five parts: a Drive trigger, a Steady perk, a Fraying perk, a hook, and a Breaking fallout. Match the twelve below for strength: a once-per-scene or once-per-round limit on any perk that would otherwise fire on most rolls, and a Breaking fallout that costs the hero something real.

**Justice**

- *Drive:* when you do the right thing instead of the easy thing.
- *Steady:* Edge on rolls to stop someone hurting an innocent.
- *Fraying:* that Edge becomes Advantage. *Hook:* you won't let a guilty person walk, even when chasing them costs the team.
- *Breaking:* you go after the guilty party alone, ignoring orders and danger.

**Vengeance**

- *Drive:* when you go after someone who hurt you or yours.
- *Steady:* Edge against anyone who has hurt you this Night.
- *Fraying:* +1 Harm on hits against them. *Hook:* you can't let them get away.
- *Breaking:* you go too far. The GM asks how far, and you answer honestly. Take +1 Heat.

**Mercy**

- *Drive:* when you risk yourself to save someone, even an enemy.
- *Steady:* when you Help an ally, they get Advantage instead of Edge.
- *Fraying:* once per scene, clear 1 Stress when you pull someone out of danger. *Hook:* you can't leave anyone in danger, enemies included.
- *Breaking:* you automatically take every hit aimed at anyone in your zone until the scene ends.

**Spite**

- *Drive:* when you get even.
- *Steady:* when someone hurts you, your next attack on them has Edge.
- *Fraying:* whenever you take Harm, your attacker takes 1 Stress, once per round. *Hook:* you can't back down from an insult or a challenge.
- *Breaking:* you attack whoever is closest, friend or foe.

**Order**

- *Drive:* when the team follows a plan you made.
- *Steady:* when you lay out a plan, name one step of it. The roll for that step has Edge, whoever makes it. Once per scene.
- *Fraying:* that Edge becomes Advantage. *Hook:* when the plan falls apart, you have Disadvantage until you spend a Full Action making a new one.
- *Breaking:* you freeze or double down on a plan that's clearly failing. The GM picks which.

**Vanity**

- *Drive:* when you show off in front of witnesses.
- *Steady:* Edge on Presence rolls when people are watching.
- *Fraying:* +1 Harm on a hit made in front of witnesses, once per round. *Hook:* take +1 Heat any time you act in public.
- *Breaking:* you grandstand. Each turn you do the most impressive thing available, even if it leaves your team exposed.

**Guilt**

- *Drive:* when you take the blame or the pain for something that isn't your fault.
- *Steady:* clear Bruised when you protect someone.
- *Fraying:* Advantage when protecting someone like the person you failed. *Hook:* you can't turn down someone who asks for help.
- *Breaking:* you shut down and lose your next turn. Then you confess something true to someone who shouldn't hear it.

**Thrill**

- *Drive:* when you pick the riskier option.
- *Steady:* once per fight, Strain costs no Stress.
- *Fraying:* Advantage on a Flow roll, once per round. *Hook:* you won't take cover or retreat.
- *Breaking:* you do the most dangerous thing in reach and drag someone into it with you. You still go after enemies, but you drop caution and stop protecting your friends.

**Loyalty**

- *Drive:* when you take a risk for a connection or a teammate.
- *Steady:* once per scene, name one roll you make to protect, help or avenge one of your connections or a teammate. That roll has Edge.
- *Fraying:* that Edge becomes Advantage. *Hook:* you won't leave anyone behind or sell anyone out.
- *Breaking:* you shield them from the truth or the law, even when they're wrong: you lie, hide evidence, or step between them and justice. Take +1 Heat.

**Obsession**

- *Drive:* when you chase your target past what's sensible.
- *Steady:* at the start of each Night, name one person or case as your target. Once per scene, name one roll that moves it forward. That roll has Edge.
- *Fraying:* that Edge becomes Advantage. *Hook:* you ignore anything that isn't about the target.
- *Breaking:* you abandon the team or a civilian to chase the lead. The GM picks which.

**Fear**

- *Drive:* when you act despite being scared.
- *Steady:* Edge on the first roll you make in each fight.
- *Fraying:* once per scene, clear 1 Stress when an attack against you Misses. *Hook:* you overprepare and won't go in blind.
- *Breaking:* you run, or you strike first and hardest at the thing you're afraid of. The GM picks which.

**Duty**

- *Drive:* when you keep your word or follow your code at a cost.
- *Steady:* once per scene, name one roll you make to keep an oath, a promise or your code. That roll has Edge.
- *Fraying:* that Edge becomes Advantage. *Hook:* you won't break your code, even when it's tactically dumb.
- *Breaking:* you follow the code into disaster, whoever it costs.

## Drive

Drive is your heroic fuel. There is one price list for everyone. Archetypes change how you earn Drive, never what it costs.

**You hold up to 6 Drive.** Start your first Night with 2. At Dawn, Drive above 3 drops to 3, and Drive below 2 comes back up to 2.

### Earning Drive

| Source | Gain | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your archetype trigger | 1 | Once per round |
| Your Trait's Drive trigger | 1 | Once per scene |
| Going along with the GM's hook while Fraying | 1 | Each time |
| Protecting one of your connections (see Connections) | 1 | Once per scene |
| Breaking (at the end of the scene) | 2 | Each time |

Finishing an Objective step counts as your archetype trigger for that round. Some Flairs and powers add their own sources (Opportunist, I can take it, Reveal).

In a fight, expect to earn about 1 Drive every two rounds.

### Spending Drive

| Cost | Effect | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Edge** (+2) | Before you roll |
| 2 | **Upgrade:** raise a hit by one Impact level (1 to 2, or 2 to 3; Impact 3 is the top) | After any roll that hits (attacks, leads, Crack the Case), once per roll |
| 3 | **Signature Move** | On your turn, as its own action (see Archetypes) |
| 3 | **Second Wind:** clear one Harm box, no roll | A Full Action, once per fight. For a Downed hero it takes the whole turn and puts them back up at Wounded |

You can spend Drive on an ally's roll for Edge if you're in the same zone and can say how you're helping.

## Fights

Fights happen on a map of 3 to 6 **zones**: named areas like *Loading Dock*, *Catwalk*, *Back Office*. Draw them as boxes on paper and connect the ones you can move between. No grid, no counting squares.

### Zones and range

Give each zone 0 to 2 tags: **Cover**, **High Ground**, **Dark**, **Cramped**, **Hazard** (fire, traffic, a live wire), **Crowd**.

| Range | Means | Who can hit you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engaged | Toe to toe with someone in your zone | Melee and point-blank shots |
| Near | Same zone, not engaged | Guns, thrown things, Presence |
| Far | An adjacent zone | Guns at Disadvantage (long guns and bows ignore this) |
| Out of reach | Two or more zones away | Nobody, unless a power says so |

**Next to** someone means in the same zone as them. Stabilizing, a med kit and freeing a cuffed ally all work from anywhere in the zone.

**What the tags do:**

- **Cover:** Take Cover (Quick Action) gives +2 Guard until your next turn. You can Vanish here.
- **Dark:** ranged attacks in or into the zone are at Disadvantage. You can Vanish here.
- **Crowd:** you can Vanish here. Guns fired into a Crowd risk hitting bystanders (a fair GM move on any miss).
- **Hazard:** when you tag a zone Hazard, name its type: fire, gas, a fall, hooks. Any missed roll in this zone costs a flat 1 Harm, heroes and villains alike, and Toughness doesn't reduce it. Some types add one more effect (see Hazard types, below), and gear and powers that name a type work as written, like a rebreather for gas or a glider cape for a fall.
- **High Ground:** ranged attacks from it get Edge. Melee can't reach you unless the attacker climbs up first, which is a move or, on a hard climb, a roll.
- **Cramped:** heavy melee and long guns are at Disadvantage, and no more than two people can be engaged with any one person. A good spot for one hero to hold off a gang.

**Hazard types (trial).** Some types add one more effect. Gear and powers that name a type (a rebreather, a glider cape, Acrobat) work as written.

| Type | Also |
| --- | --- |
| Fire | At the end of round 2, it spreads to one adjacent zone unless someone puts it out. |
| Gas or smoke | The zone also counts as Dark. |
| A drop (a catwalk, a roof edge) | Knocking someone off it (a shove at Impact 2 or 3, a Haymaker, Hurl) costs them the flat 1 Harm, no miss needed. |
| Traffic or a stampede | Leaving the zone takes your free move and a Quick Action. |
| Anything else (hooks, live wires) | Nothing extra. |

**Hidden (trial).** Hidden ends when you attack, when you leave the zone tags that allow it (or they go away), or when an enemy spends a Quick Action to search and beats your Flow-based roll: they roll Focus against 12 + your Flow. Mooks search with their attack bonus.

### Turn order

Sides alternate: one hero, then one villain or mook group, then another hero, and so on. The players pick which hero goes next. If one side runs out of turns, the other side finishes theirs. Heroes take the first turn of every round, unless they were ambushed: then the enemy takes the first turn of round 1 only. Whoever ambushes has Edge on one roll: the first roll by whoever acts first on their side.

At the **end of each round**, tick any clocks in play (a hostage, a fuse, sirens getting closer).

### Your turn

You get **one free move** (to an adjacent zone, where you can also engage one enemy there (trial), or into or out of engagement in your zone), plus **one Full Action or two Quick Actions**.

**Full Actions**

- **Strike:** attack with Force, Flow, or Presence. Normal Impact. A heavy melee attack is a Strike with Force (trial): swinging a bat takes muscle, not speed.
- **Signature Move:** costs 3 Drive (see Archetypes).
- **Stabilize:** save a Dying ally next to you.

**Quick Actions**

- **Jab:** a quick attack at -2 (no penalty with light melee). A hit is always Impact 1, so bonuses that need Impact 2 or better (the light melee bonus, guns) never apply to a Jab. Once per turn.
- **Move:** go one more zone.
- **Help:** give an ally in your zone Edge on their next roll.
- **Take Cover:** in a Cover zone, +2 Guard until your next turn.
- **Interact:** pick up, open, throw a switch, kick a gun away.
- **Archetype Quick Actions:** listed under each archetype.

**Breaking away:** if you leave engagement, the enemy you were engaged with can make a free Jab at you.

**Leaving a fight (trial):** from a zone with an exit, when you aren't engaged, spend your turn to leave. If you're engaged, break away first as usual, then spend your turn. Rooftop Runner skips the turn cost.

**Chases (trial).** When someone runs and someone follows, each round the chasers pick one hero to roll Flow against the quarry's Guard (a car or a rooftop route uses a rating instead). Three hits catch them, and an Impact 3 counts as two. Three Misses and they're gone. When the heroes are the ones running, they roll the same way against the pursuers' Wits: three hits and they're clear.

**Vehicles in a fight (trial).** A moving vehicle is a zone, and the road around it is a Hazard zone (traffic). Whoever drives spends a Quick Action each turn to keep it on the road. Getting from one moving vehicle to another is a Flow roll against a rating: -1 in slow traffic, +1 at speed, +3 on the highway. Shooting from a moving vehicle is at Disadvantage if you're also the driver. Ramming is a Full Action attack by the driver: roll with the vehicle's rating in place of Force (+1 for a bike, +3 for a car or a truck) against the target's Guard. A hit counts as heavy melee, and at Impact 2 or 3 it also shoves the target into an adjacent zone. A vehicle has no Harm boxes: it is wrecked by a hard move, or by an Impact 3 attack aimed at it.

**Maneuvers (trial).** Grab, disarm, shove or trip someone you're engaged with. A maneuver is a Full Action, or a Quick Action at -2 like a Jab (and like a Jab, a hit counts as Impact 1). Roll Force to grab or shove, or Flow to trip or disarm, against the target's Guard. A maneuver deals no Harm, and like any attack it never carries a cost. Against a mook, a hit just takes it out of the fight.

| Maneuver | Impact 1 | Impact 2 or 3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grab | You hold them. Neither of you can take a free move or leave the zone until the grab ends. | As Impact 1, and allies' melee attacks against them have Edge. |
| Disarm | Their weapon bonus is off until they spend a Quick Action to get their grip back. | The weapon lands in the zone, or in your hand. |
| Shove or trip | They lose their next free move. | Push them into an adjacent zone, or off High Ground. |

**Breaking a grab:** a Quick Action, Force or Flow against 12 + the grabber's Force. The grab also ends if the grabber attacks someone else, moves, or goes Down. A named enemy breaks free at the end of their next turn, no roll. Powers that give Edge to grab, hold or disarm (Martial Artist, Peak Strength) apply to these rolls. Powers that give Edge on escapes (Elasticity, Sand) apply to breaking a grab.

### Strain

Once per turn, take 1 Stress to get an extra Quick Action.

### Enemies

Villains and mooks roll 2d10 + their stat against your defense, the same way you do. Mooks act in groups of up to 3. Each group makes one attack per turn, with Edge if all 3 are still standing.

**Morale (trial).** A gang runs when half of its mooks are down, unless a named enemy is in sight. Mooks with nowhere to run give up instead. Fanatics, the mind-controlled, animals and swarms never run. Cops don't run either: they hold until the next group arrives. The GM can always give a gang a reason to stay: a debt, a hostage of their own, a boss they fear more than the heroes.

**Lieutenants:** a lieutenant gets one turn and one free Presence attack per round, whatever the party size (see Villains, Scaling to the party).

**Hard-to-hit enemies:** a defense of 15 or more (a dodgy gunman at Flow +3) means a +3 hero misses about half the time. That's the point: those enemies are out-thought, not out-punched. Use one per fight at most, and make sure the fight offers a way to get Edge or Advantage: Read, Help, Hidden, a Flashbang, High Ground.

### Give the fight a goal (trial)

Every Confrontation has an **Objective** of 2 to 4 steps, written in case prep. It is the goal the fight's clock is counting down against: a hostage, a getaway van, evidence burning, cops on the way. If both sides can just slug it out, the fight runs long and the zones stop mattering.

- **A step** is a Full Action and a roll, with whatever stat fits how you go about it, against a rating (see Obstacles: -1, +1 or +3). Say where it has to happen: a padlocked door, a console on the Catwalk.
- **Results:** use the core roll. Impact 2 or 3 finishes the step clean, and Impact 3 also gives an opening (Edge on the next step). Impact 1 finishes it with a cost: the GM offers two and you pick one. A Miss leaves the step undone, and the GM makes a soft move (see GM moves).
- **Any hero can take any step,** in any order, unless the GM says they come in sequence.
- **Every step pays.** When the GM writes a step, they also write what finishing it changes right away: the clock stops for a round, a zone gains or loses a tag, a mook group loses heart and runs. A half-finished Objective should still have been worth it.
- **Steps earn Drive.** Finishing a step counts as your archetype's Drive trigger for that round (1 Drive, once per round as usual).
- **You can't work a step while engaged,** unless the step is about that enemy (talking them down, taking something off them). Someone has to hold them off.
- **Finishing the Objective** ends the fight in the heroes' favour as surely as dropping the villain.

Objectives are where non-fighters earn their spot: grabbing the evidence, holding a door, talking a hostage-taker down, or disarming a device ends a fight as decisively as a knockout.

### Defeat (trial)

When every hero is Down or has fled, the heroes have lost the fight. The villain gets what they came for, the Scheme clock ticks twice, and each hero ticks Home or Heat (their choice). Nobody dies from defeat alone: a Dying hero is stabilized by a bystander, or because the villain walks away. They stay Down until Dawn, as usual.

### Conditions at a glance

A reference. The rules themselves are in Harm, Stress and Traits, Zones and range, Maneuvers, and Cops and Crackdowns.

| Condition | What it does | How it ends |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hidden | Enemies can't target you. Your first attack from Hidden has Advantage. | You attack, you leave the tags that allow it, or a search beats 12 + your Flow. |
| Grabbed | No free move, and you can't leave the zone. | A Quick Action: Force or Flow against 12 + the grabber's Force. |
| Cuffed | You can't act, except to slip them. | A Full Action, Force or Flow against 13, or an ally's Full Action. |
| Wounded | Disadvantage on Force and Flow. Grit your teeth: 1 Stress ignores it until your next turn. | Patch Up at Dawn, a med kit, or Second Wind. |
| Down | Out of the fight: talk, crawl, or one desperate Quick Action a round at Disadvantage. | Up at Wounded when the fight ends, or by Second Wind or a med kit. |
| Dying | Hit again while Down. An ally's Full Action stabilizes you. | Unstabilized at the scene's end: you die or are left for dead (the player decides). |
| Fraying | 4 to 7 Stress. The Fraying perk replaces Steady, and the GM gets your hook. | Back to 3 Stress or less. |
| Breaking | 8 Stress. Your Breaking fallout runs the scene. | At the scene's end: down to 4 Stress, +2 Drive, Home ticks once. |

## The Night

A session is one Night: **Patrol**, then **Confrontation**, then **Dawn**. Dawn is where the costs of the mask catch up with you.

&#91;embedded content: one Night · 3 phases, repeated until the case closes\]

Not every Night ends in the big fight. Early Nights usually end with a smaller fight against mooks or a lieutenant.

### Patrol: working the case

Investigation never stalls. If your approach fits a lead, you get the clue. The roll only decides what it costs you.

**Case prep (GM, about 15 minutes):**

- **Three facets:** Who's behind it? What do they want? Where will they strike?
- **The cast:** 4 to 6 suspects, factions, or players, each with a motive and something to hide.
- **Clues:** Complexity plus 4 of them. Concrete facts (a burned ledger page, a van with fake plates, a dockworker who quit the same week). Each one should fit more than one suspect.
- **3 to 5 leads:** places and people the heroes can go lean on.
- **Complexity (trial):** 4 for a one-Night job. A normal case is 2 plus twice the number of heroes: 6 for two heroes (or one), 8 for three, 10 for four. An arc finale is 2 more than a normal case. More heroes find more clues each Night, so a bigger team needs more of them.
- **A Scheme clock:** 6 segments (see below).
- **Scheme beats (trial):** three, at 1, 3 and 5 on the clock. Each is an event the heroes can see in the fiction: a witness disappears, a building burns. At 6 the villain pulls it off.

**Low prep (trial).** That list is the most a case needs, not the least. Driven is built to be improvised. Most cases need only the villain: decide who is behind it, or leave even that open (see Floating, below), and make up the rest at the table. The heroes' neighborhoods and connections supply the people and places (see Your neighborhood), and Free-form Patrol hands out the clues.

**Two ways to run a case.** The GM picks for each case:

- **Fixed Culprit (default).** The GM decides who's behind it before play. Everything else (what they want, where they'll strike, how it connects) comes from the players' theory. Use this for recurring villains, secret identities, or any idea the GM wants to land.
- **Floating.** Nobody knows who did it, the GM included. The theory decides everything. Good for early sessions or when the GM wants to improvise. Clues have to stay open enough to point several ways.

A campaign can start Floating and move to Fixed Culprit once recurring villains show up. Switching between cases is fine.

**Following a lead:** describe your approach and roll the stat that fits against the lead's rating. You get the clue no matter what.

| Result | You get |
| --- | --- |
| Miss | The clue, and the GM picks a cost: tick the Scheme clock, +1 Heat, 1 Stress, or an ambush |
| Impact 1 | The clue, and the GM offers two of these costs; you pick one |
| Impact 2 | The clue, clean |
| Impact 3 | The clue, plus one: a second clue, Edge on one roll against the villain in the showdown, or a hint at their Weakness |

**Patrol pool (trial).** Each Night the team shares a pool of Patrol actions equal to the number of heroes, minimum 2. Following a lead costs one action, and it buys everything that comes of that lead: any fight, search or interrogation in that thread costs no further action.

**Clues and leads (trial).** Each lead gives one clue, plus the Impact 3 bonus as written. Anything else the heroes find in a scene is a new lead, not a clue. The team keeps it, and it becomes a clue when someone spends a Patrol action on it, that Night or a later one.

**One no-roll clue per Night (trial).** The whole team takes at most one no-roll clue per Night, from all sources combined: Work the Case, Plugs, Network, a forensic kit, a hacking rig and the like. Any other no-roll source used that Night gives Edge on one lead roll instead. If that happens at Dawn, the Edge carries to the next Night's first lead.

**Free-form Patrol (optional, trial).** The Patrol pool, lead rolls and the one no-roll clue are tools, not requirements. The GM can set them aside and let clues and leads turn up through play: the heroes go where they want and talk to who they want, and the GM hands over a clue whenever a scene earns one. Two things stay, so the case keeps its pressure. The Scheme clock still ticks at every Dawn, and when a scene goes badly, the GM picks a cost from the lead table or makes a Patrol move (see GM moves). As a guide, about one clue per hero per Night keeps the Complexity numbers right. Crack the Case works the same either way, and the GM can switch styles from one Night to the next.

**Interrogation (trial).** Interrogating a captured named enemy is a roll of Presence against their Poise. It costs no action when the capture came out of a lead the team already paid for.

| Result | You get |
| --- | --- |
| Miss | The clue, and the GM picks a cost |
| Impact 1 | The clue, and the GM offers two costs; you pick one |
| Impact 2 | The clue, and the Scheme clock doesn't tick at the next Dawn |
| Impact 3 | As Impact 2, plus the usual Impact 3 bonus |

Only a lieutenant can stop the tick, and only once per case. An accomplice gives the clue only.

**Tailing and sneaking in (trial).** These need no rules of their own. Tailing someone is a Chase: three hits and you followed them all the way unseen, three Misses and they spot you or lose you. Getting into a place unseen is a lead roll, or an Objective step when it happens during a fight.

**The Scheme clock** is the villain's plan moving forward. It ticks at every Dawn and whenever a cost says so. When it reaches 1, 3 or 5, that beat happens. If it fills at 6 before you crack the case, the villain pulls it off: the investigation stops, and the showdown happens on their ground, after the damage is done.

**One deadline per case (trial).** The Scheme clock is the only timer. A date the villain names is flavor. When you write clues, give deadlines as relative triggers ("after the inspection"), not calendar dates.

**Personal clues.** Put 1 or 2 clues in each case that hit close to home. Early on, pull them from backstories: the victim is Rook's old sparring partner. Once the campaign is moving, pull them from **earlier issues**: a villain who got away, a witness you saved, a promise you broke, a Breaking scene that came back around. A personal clue counts as **2 clues** in a theory, but using it ticks that hero's Home clock. The case followed you home.

**Arc clues.** Some clues point past this case to whoever is pulling strings across the arc. They don't count toward the current case. Keep them on their own list for the arc's finale.

**Drive while investigating** (1 Drive each): call in a contact for a new lead, ask the GM one yes-or-no question about a clue, or flash back to something you set up earlier (a tracker, a bribe, a bug in the office).

### Cracking the case

When the team holds clues equal to at least half the Complexity and agrees on a theory that answers all three facets, roll **2d10 + clues used against 10 + Complexity**. Clues that don't fit the theory don't count. Call them red herrings.

| Result | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Miss | Wrong. The GM says which facet doesn't hold up. The team picks one: **Regroup** (the Scheme clock ticks twice; try again once you have at least one new clue) or **Go in anyway** (the Scheme clock ticks once, and you walk into a trap). |
| Impact 1 | Right, with a snag: they're ready for you, or it's the right place but the wrong night |
| Impact 2 | Right |
| Impact 3 | Right, and you also learn the villain's Weakness or gain an arc clue |

With clues equal to the Complexity, the theory is right 64% of the time. Two extra clues raises that to 79%. Going in with half is a long shot, and longer for a big team: 36% at Complexity 6, 28% at 8, 21% at 10.

**The trap (trial).** Going in on a wrong theory is a Confrontation on the villain's ground, and their side ambushes. The case villain isn't there unless the Scheme clock is full: the trap is a lieutenant or a gang. The GM shapes it around the facet that was wrong:

- **Wrong Who:** you corner a patsy as a clean-up crew arrives to silence them. The Objective is keeping the patsy alive.
- **Wrong What:** you walk into a live job. Take +1 Heat, and the cops arrive on round 2.
- **Wrong Where:** a decoy. One zone is a Hazard, and a clock of 4 counts down to something the heroes have to get clear of.

Win the fight or finish its Objective and the team takes 1 clue, and the GM gives the true answer to the facet that was wrong. Lose it and Defeat applies as usual.

**Fixed Culprit and the wrong name:** if the theory hits but names the wrong person, the plan and the place are still right. The person you named was working for the real culprit, who gets away clean and becomes a lead for a later case.

### Confrontation: whose ground?

- **Impact 2 or 3:** you choose the ground. Ambush the villain (one roll on your side has Edge: see Turn order), or add one zone tag of your choice to the map.
- **Impact 1:** you know where, but they're ready. No ambush either way, and the villain starts with +1 Drive.
- **A trap, or a full Scheme clock:** the villain chooses the ground and gets the ambush.

### Dawn: the cost of the mask

At the start of Dawn, the Scheme clock ticks and its Stress lands, before heroes make their picks. A hero who is still Down takes none of it (see How Stress builds).

Each hero picks **two**:

- **Sleep It Off:** clear 3 Stress.
- **Patch Up:** clear Hurt and Wounded. Pick both this and Sleep It Off to clear Down.
- **Show Up:** be there for your civilian life. Play a short scene with one of your connections, then clear 1 Stress. If you don't pick this, tick your Home clock.
- **Lay Low:** clear 1 Heat.
- **Work the Case:** chase one lead off-screen and take 1 clue, no roll. It counts as the team's one no-roll clue for the Night. If that is already used, take Edge on a lead next Night instead.

A Lair with the Gym style adds a sixth pick, Train (see The Lair).

You can never do everything. That's the point.

### The clocks

| Clock | Size | Ticks when | At half | When it fills |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Scheme** (team) | 6 | Every Dawn, and as a cost when following leads | Beats at 1, 3 and 5 (trial): three events the heroes can see, written in case prep (a witness disappears, a building burns) | The villain pulls it off. The investigation stops, and the showdown happens on their ground, after the damage is done. |
| **Heat** (team) | 6 | Guns (and being seen with a long gun or a shotgun), public fights, collateral damage, Vanity, Vengeance, sloppy feeding (Vampirism on Impact 1 or a miss), a Marked hero seen by civilians | Cops show up to fights on round 3 and try to arrest everyone, heroes included (see Cops and Crackdowns) | Crackdown: a task force, a smear in the press, or someone gets a description. Play it out, then drop Heat to 2. |
| **Home** (each hero) | 6 | You skip Show Up, you Break, you use a personal clue, you use Resources to buy past a problem, or a villain finds your Lair (+2) | Someone close starts asking questions | Home crisis: lose the job, the relationship ends, someone learns who you are, or a loved one becomes a target. It always costs or changes one of that hero's Connections (trial). Play it out, then reset to 0. |

**Heroes with no civilian life.** A pure vigilante has no day job or alter ego. Their civilian life, and so Show Up and the Home clock, just stands for whatever anchors them to their humanity, even if it's only feeding a goldfish. Name it when you build the hero. When Home ticks, that anchor is what's slipping, and a Home crisis is that anchor being lost, found or threatened.

### Between cases (trial)

When a case closes, time passes: days, maybe a few weeks. Before the next case starts:

- **Harm** clears completely, a Dying mark included.
- **Stress** above 3 drops to 3. Lower Stress stays where it is. A hero with Already Broken stays at 4.
- **Drive** goes back to what you start a first Night with.
- **Heat** drops by 2. The city has a short memory, but not that short.
- **Home** doesn't move. Only a Home crisis resets it.
- **The Scheme clock** goes away with the case. A villain who got away is a lead for a later one.
- **A task force** ends with the case. A smear lasts through the next case, as written.
- Each hero takes one **Advancement** pick (see Archetypes, Advancement).

### Cops and Crackdowns (trial)

**Cops in a fight.** At Heat 3 or more, one group of Cops arrives on round 3, and another each round after.

- **They arrest.** A cop hit grabs the target instead of dealing Harm. A cop hit on someone already grabbed or Down cuffs them.
- **Cuffed:** you can't act, except a Full Action to slip them: Force or Flow against 13. An ally next to you can free you with a Full Action, no roll.
- **Fight the cops and it escalates.** The first time a hero attacks a cop, take +1 Heat. From then on the cops deal Harm as usual, and their pistols follow the gun rules.
- **Busted.** A hero still cuffed when the team leaves is arrested. They spend Dawn in a cell: no Dawn picks, so their Home clock ticks for the missed Show Up. They're out by the next Night (bail, a lawyer, a cop who owes someone).

**Crackdown.** When Heat fills, the GM picks one, plays it out, then drops Heat to 2:

- **Task force.** For the rest of the case, cops come to every fight on round 2, whatever the Heat, led by the Task Force Commander (see Sample villains). Beat him in a fight or expose him and it ends.
- **A smear.** For the next case, Presence rolls against civilians and cops are at Disadvantage.
- **A description.** Someone got a look at the build, the car, the tattoo. Every hero ticks Home by 1.

## GM moves (trial)

A move is something the GM adds to the scene that the heroes now have to deal with. Make one when a roll Misses, when a clock hits a beat or fills, and when the players hand you an opening: they split up, they ignore a threat you named, or they look at you to see what happens.

Three habits:

- **Start from the fiction.** Pick the move the scene is already pointing at. A chain put on tonight isn't rusted.
- **Say the threat first.** A soft move tells the players what is about to happen and leaves them a turn to stop it.
- **Then follow through.** If they don't stop it, it happens. That is a hard move, and they were warned.

A **soft move** makes things worse with no Harm, no Stress and no clock tick. A **hard move** costs something real, and the hero it lands on also takes 1 Stress.

### Costs

When a roll at Impact 1 comes with a cost, offer two of these and let the player pick. Attacks never carry a cost. At 8 Stress, leave Stress off and offer two others.

- 1 Harm.
- 1 Stress.
- A clock ticks (the fight's clock, the Scheme, Heat).
- You lose your spot: you end up engaged, in the open, or cut off from the team.
- You lose a thing: a weapon skitters away, a tool breaks, a charge is used up.
- One of your connections gets pulled in.

### In a fight

A Miss earns a soft move, because the enemy still gets its own turn. A hard move needs one of these reasons: the hero was warned (the GM said out loud what would happen), the roll had Disadvantage, or it is that hero's second Miss in a row. A Miss in a Hazard zone already costs 1 Harm, so it is not a reason on its own.

Soft fight moves:

- **Reposition.** An enemy takes Cover, climbs to High Ground, or cuts off an exit.
- **Close in.** An enemy engages a hero who wanted to stay clear, or pins one in a Cramped zone.
- **Backup.** A mook group arrives next round. Say where from.
- **Move the stake.** The hostage is dragged a zone away, the evidence is picked up, the van starts.
- **Change the ground.** The lights go out, a fire spreads, a crowd pours in. A zone gains a tag.
- **Name the next hit.** Say who the villain is going for and how. If nobody stops it, the villain's attack on their next turn has Edge.

Hard fight moves:

- **Hurt.** An enemy gets a free Jab at the hero (armor and Toughness apply).
- **Tick.** A clock in play ticks.
- **Strip.** The hero is disarmed, or one piece of their gear is smashed until Dawn.
- **Separate.** A door slams, a floor gives way, smoke rolls in. The hero needs a Quick Action to get back to the team.
- **Take.** The villain gets something they came for: a step of their own plan, a hostage in hand, an exit.
- **Finish them.** An enemy goes for a hero who is Down. This is the hardest move in the game. Only a killer does it, and the GM always says it out loud first. It leaves the hero Dying. Whether they die is still their player's call (see Harm, Death is decided at the table).

Hard moves follow the hero's Trait when they can. A Fraying hero's hook is always a fair soft move.

### On Patrol

A missed lead keeps the costs in its own table. Use these for a Miss on any other Patrol roll, and when a Scheme beat lands.

Soft Patrol moves:

- **Clam up.** A witness goes quiet, or a contact wants a favor first.
- **Eyes on you.** A tail, a camera, a parked car with the engine running.
- **Beaten to it.** The person has moved on, the place has been picked over.
- **Two ways.** The clue fits two suspects, and it takes one more fact to choose.
- **A knock at the door.** Someone from a hero's connections shows up at the worst time.
- **Show the villain's hand.** The heroes see the next Scheme beat coming and can spend a Patrol action to get in its way.

Hard Patrol moves:

- **Tick.** The Scheme clock ticks.
- **Heat.** +1 Heat: a description, a phone video, a cop who remembers a face.
- **Ambush.** A fight starts on their ground.
- **Burn a source.** A contact, a lead or a tool is gone for the rest of the case.
- **Lean on a connection.** Someone with leverage over the hero makes a call. Tick that hero's Home clock.
- **Make it personal.** The trouble lands on someone the hero protects. They are where the next beat happens.

### At Dawn

Dawn has no rolls, so it has no Misses. Use these when a hero skips Show Up, when a Home clock passes half, and when Heat passes half.

- **Someone noticed.** A bruise, a missed shift, a lie that didn't hold. A connection asks a straight question.
- **The bill comes due.** Whoever has leverage over the hero wants something by tomorrow night.
- **The city talks.** A headline, a rumor, a sketch on the news. Say what people now believe about the heroes.
- **Empty chair.** Something happened in the hero's civilian life while they were out, and they weren't there for it.

## Archetypes

### Character creation checklist

Follow these steps in order. Each one points to the section that has the rules.

1. **Stat array.** Pick Specialist or Balanced and assign it to Force, Flow, Focus and Presence (Stats and defenses, Starting stats).
2. **Trait.** Pick one (Stress and Traits, Traits).
3. **Archetype.** Pick one (Archetypes, below).
4. **Flair.** Pick one (Archetypes, Flairs).
5. **Four Connections.** Write one line each, and link at least one to another hero (Archetypes, Connections).
6. **Free weapon.** Pick one. You also carry a knife (Gear and Powers, Weapons you carry).
7. **Three slots.** Spend them on gear, a street power or a strange power (Gear and Powers). Specialists and Gadgeteers also fill a utility belt.
8. **Anchor for Home.** Name your civilian life: a job, a family, an alter ego. A hero with no civilian life names whatever anchors them to their humanity (The Night, The clocks, Heroes with no civilian life).
9. **Neighborhood (trial).** Name your hero's neighborhood, who runs it, and what's wrong there right now (Archetypes, Your neighborhood).
10. **Team pick (optional, trial).** If your table uses this rule, the team chooses it together once every hero is built: a Lair, with its style (Gym, Hideout, Front or Watchtower), or any one Asset (Gear and Powers, The Lair).

### The six archetypes

Your archetype is how you fight and how you earn Drive. Each one has a **Drive trigger** (once per round), a **feature**, and a **Signature Move** (3 Drive, Full Action).

### Protector

You stand between people and harm.

- **Drive trigger (once per round):** an enemy attacks you (hit or miss), or you take a hit meant for someone else.
- **Interpose** (Quick): until your next turn, when an enemy attacks someone in your zone, you can make yourself the target instead. Once per round.
- **Signature: Stand Your Ground.** Until your next turn, every attack in your zone has to target you, you get +1 Toughness (max 2), and allies in your zone get Edge on their next roll.

### Brawler

You wade in and keep swinging.

- **Drive trigger (once per round):** you take Harm, or you drop an enemy.
- **Pile Through:** when your Strike drops a mook, make a free Jab against another enemy in your zone.
- **Signature: Haymaker.** A Strike with Advantage and +1 Harm. On a hit, knock the target into an adjacent zone of your choice.

### Stalker

You strike from the dark and are gone before they turn around.

- **Drive trigger (once per round):** you attack while Hidden, or you break away from engagement without getting hit.
- **Vanish** (Quick): in a Dark, Cover, or Crowd zone, roll Flow against the highest Wits of anyone watching. On a hit you're **Hidden**: you can't be targeted, and your first attack from Hidden has Advantage (attacking ends Hidden).
- **Signature: Take Down.** A Strike from Hidden. If it hits, it counts as Impact 3.

### Specialist

Gadgets, trick shots, and a plan for everything.

- **Drive trigger (once per round):** you use a tool to change the fight (a new zone tag, a moved enemy, a blocked exit).
- **Utility belt:** 3 belt slots, on top of your 3 slots. At the start of each Night, fill them with tools or light armor (any one-slot gear that isn't a weapon). Belt gear works exactly like gear. Expecting guns? Take smoke grenades or a Kevlar vest: without them, a Specialist is usually the first one down.
- **Signature: Prepared.** Reveal something you set up earlier: a new exit, a trap (Impact 2 to whoever sets it off, ignoring Toughness), or backup arriving. Then take Advantage on your next roll.

### Detective

You read people and see the fight coming.

- **Drive trigger (once per round):** you learn something true about an enemy, you find a clue, or an ally hits using the Edge from your Read.
- **Case Sense:** once per case, one clue you found counts as two in the team's theory.
- **Read** (Quick, in a fight): roll Focus against a target's Wits. For each Impact level, ask one: *What's their weakness? What will they do next? What are they protecting?* The GM answers truthfully, and your team's next roll against that target has Edge. If you asked about their weakness, a hit with that Edge also ignores Toughness: you showed them where to strike.
- **Signature: Called It.** Name what a villain will do next. When they try it, it fails, and every ally gets a free Quick Action.

### Leader (trial)

You make everyone around you better, and you carry what it costs them. Presence is your stat: a Presence Strike takes a mook out like any other hit, and deals Stress to a named enemy.

- **Drive trigger (once per round):** an ally hits with Edge you gave them (Rally or Help), or your Presence attack hits a named enemy.
- **Carry the weight:** once per scene, when an ally who can hear you would take Stress, you take it instead.
- **Rally** (Quick): pick one ally who can hear you, in any zone. They get Edge on their next roll, or they make a free move right now. Once per round.
- **Signature: On My Signal.** Pick one ally who can hear you. They take a full turn right now (a free move, plus one Full Action or two Quick Actions), with Advantage on their first roll. It doesn't use up their own turn this round.

### Flairs

Pick one at character creation.

- **The Best:** when you go Down, take one Full Action first.
- **Hard to Kill:** add a fifth Harm box, *Battered*, between Hurt and Wounded.
- **A Thing or Two:** Edge on Patrol rolls in your own neighborhood.
- **Plugs:** once per Night, a contact gives you 1 clue, no roll. Each use ticks your Home clock or Heat (your choice).
- **Opportunist:** once per scene, when an enemy misses you, gain 1 Drive.
- **Fast Hands:** once per turn, Interact is free.

**Resources** (trial Flair): money, a company, or a rich patron. At each Dawn you can swap any of your gear for other gear from the list. Once per Night, buying your way past a problem (a bribe, a rented space, a lawyer) counts as Impact 2 with no roll, but ticks Home by 1: someone in your daytime life notices.

**Acrobat** (trial Flair): Hazard zones don't cost you Harm on a miss, and you can move into a zone and climb to its High Ground in the same free move.

**Already Broken** (trial Flair): your Stress never drops below 4, so you're always Fraying and the GM always has a hook. In return, start each Night with 4 Drive instead of 2.

**The Roast** (trial Flair): once per fight, as a Quick Action, roll Presence against a named enemy's Poise. On a hit, they take Stress equal to the Impact and the next attack against them has Edge. A Miss costs you 1 Stress: it landed badly.

**Patient Hunter** (trial Flair): once per Night, when you take a clue, take it clean: no cost is paid, even on a Miss.

**Sucker Punch** (trial Flair): your first attack against an enemy who hasn't acted yet has Edge.

**Bodyguard** (trial Flair): once per scene, take a hit aimed at an ally in your zone. You take 1 less Harm from it.

**Quick Study** (trial Flair): once per scene, after a villain spends Drive on a Trick against you, you have Edge against that villain for the rest of the scene.

**Chill** (trial Flair): once per fight, ignore one Stress you would gain, or clear 1 Stress when you roll Impact 3.

**Rooftop Runner** (trial Flair): your free move can cross one extra zone, and leaving a fight costs you no turn.

**Smarmy** (trial Flair): once per Night, a Presence roll to talk someone down or past a door counts as one Impact higher (still capped at 3).

**Scrapper** (trial Flair): once per scene, when you're outnumbered in your zone, your next attack has Edge and deals +1 Harm.

**Trick Arrows** (trial Flair): you need a bow as one of your weapons. Twice per fight, add one effect to a bow attack, a different arrow each time. Net: the target can't take their free move until they spend a Quick Action to get free (no roll). Flash: the target has Disadvantage on their next roll. Grapple: you move to any zone you can see, including High Ground. Boom: on a hit, everyone else in the target's zone takes 1 Harm.

### Connections

Every hero is tied to the world by four connections, written at character creation, one line each. They are what the GM can lean on and what the player has to lose.

- **Someone you protect.** This is your stake. A person you would take a hit for, or can't afford to lose.
- **Someone with leverage over you.** A debt, a secret, a boss, a cop, a promise. This is the GM's lever.
- **A place you're tied to.** A block, a bar, a rooftop, a grave.
- **A mistake that could come back.** An old case gone wrong, someone you failed.

At least one connection should link you to another hero. At the table, ask: how do we know each other?

The GM uses connections for Impact 1 costs, Miss moves, Home crises (a loved one becomes a target, someone learns who you are) and personal clues, which already count double and tick your Home clock. When a connection is lost, resolved or betrayed, write a new one at the next Dawn.

**Protecting a connection pays.** The first time each scene you take Harm or Stress, or put yourself at real risk, to protect or save one of your connections, gain 1 Drive.

A hero with no civilian life still has connections. Their "someone you protect" can be anything that anchors them to their humanity, even a goldfish.

### Your neighborhood (trial)

The city has no map until the table draws one. Before the first Night, each player names one neighborhood and says three things about it:

- **What it is.** One line: the docks, a hill of old money, six blocks of tower housing.
- **Who runs it.** A gang, a landlord, a precinct captain, a church, nobody.
- **What's wrong there right now.** A rumor, a string of fires, people going missing.

That is your hero's own neighborhood: where they live, work or patrol. Your "place you're tied to" connection is usually in it. Two heroes can share one. The GM adds one more that nobody claimed, for trouble to come from.

Write the names on a sheet of paper as boxes, and draw a line between any two that touch. That is the whole map.

**What it does.** Any rule that says "your neighborhood" means this one (A Thing or Two, Home turf, Reputation, the Watchtower). It is also what the GM improvises from. "Who runs it" is a faction ready to use, and "what's wrong" is a first case. When the GM needs a victim, a lead or a Scheme beat and has nothing ready, it happens in a hero's neighborhood.

**It changes.** When a case closes, each player whose neighborhood it touched says one thing that is different there now.

### Advancement

When a case closes, each hero takes one pick from this table. The limits are trial rules.

| Pick | What you get | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A stat | +1 to one stat | No stat above +4, and never two cases in a row. Your four stats never total more than +10. |
| A Flair | One new Flair | Four Flairs at most. |
| A power ability | The next locked ability on a power you have | In order: the second, then the third. |
| A slot | One new slot | Six slots at most. Fill it now with gear or a street power, or bank it: a strange power costs 2 slots, so it takes two picks. A second strange power needs the GM's agreement and its own flaw. |
| An Asset or a Lair room | One Asset, or one room for the team's Lair (see Assets) | It belongs to the team. |

**Swapping gear.** When a case closes, any hero can swap gear that fills a slot for other gear of the same cost. Powers stay.

**A new hero.** A hero who joins later, or replaces one who is gone, starts with as many picks as the teammate who has the fewest.

## Gear and Powers

Street-level heroes don't fight in street clothes. At character creation, every hero gets **3 slots** to spend:

| Pick | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| A piece of gear | 1 slot |
| A street power | 1 slot |
| A strange power | 2 slots |

A strange power plus one piece of gear is a Luke Cage or a Jessica Jones. Two street powers and a free weapon is a Daredevil. At creation, a hero can have at most one strange power. ### Assets

Assets are bigger than gear: things you can't carry into a fight, that work between scenes or across a Night. They come only from Advancement (one Asset per pick), never from Resources. An Asset belongs to the team: whoever spent the pick, everyone can use it.

- **Vehicle** (an armored car, a custom bike): one extra Patrol action per Night, Edge in chases, and once per Night it can crash into a fight as an entrance (move your whole group into any zone, and one mook group drops). Wrecking it costs a Dawn to fix.
- **Lair** (a cave, a rooftop safehouse, a back room): the team's base. At Dawn there, Sleep It Off clears 1 more Stress and Patch Up clears 1 more Harm. Later picks add rooms to it, and a villain can find it (see The Lair, below).
- **Network** (informants, a cop who trusts you, a reporter): once per Night, take a free lead with no cost. Ignore them too long and they start asking for favors.
- **Crime lab** (trial; a hidden bench, a borrowed university lab): once per Night, spend a scene on physical evidence and take 1 clue, no roll. It counts as the team's one no-roll clue. Gear that was taken from you is replaced by the next Night.
- **Back-alley clinic** (trial; a surgeon who doesn't ask names, a night-shift nurse, a vet): it clears a Dying mark at Dawn. A hero who was Dying and stabilized clears Down here with Patch Up alone.
- **Safe house** (trial; burner apartments, clean plates, spare IDs): Lay Low clears 2 Heat instead of 1. Once per case, burn an identity to ignore 1 Heat you would take.
- **Inside man** (trial; a desk sergeant, a clerk at City Hall): once per Night, Edge on a lead that runs through police or city records, and using those records adds no Heat. When Heat fills, the team picks the Crackdown instead of the GM.
- **Home turf** (trial; block captains, street vendors, a corner store): in your neighborhood, enemies can't ambush the team, and you can Vanish in a Crowd there with Edge. Once per Night there, the cops arrive a round late.

### The Lair (trial)

The Lair is the one Asset that grows. It belongs to the whole team, and any hero can spend an Advancement pick to add a room to it.

**Team pick (optional, trial).** At character creation the team gets one free shared pick: a Lair, or any one Asset. Nobody spends a slot on it. After that, the Lair and Assets grow through Advancement.

**What every Lair does.** Even with no rooms, a Lair is somewhere to rest and somewhere to hide.

- **Rest.** At Dawn there, Sleep It Off clears 1 more Stress and Patch Up clears 1 more Harm.
- **Lie low.** A hero who picks Lay Low there can't be found until the next Night: no Dawn move, ambush or arrest lands on them.
- **Meet.** The team can plan, hold a prisoner for a scene, and keep evidence there, out of sight.

**Style.** When the team founds its Lair, pick one style. It says what the place is, and gives one thing no room does.

| Style | What it is | It gives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gym | A boxing gym, a dojo, a basement full of weights | It adds a Dawn pick, **Train**: spend one of your two picks there and start the next Night with 1 more Drive. |
| Hideout | A sewer junction, a dead subway station, a boarded-up cellar | Hard to find. The first time the Lair would be found, it isn't: the threat comes to nothing. |
| Front | A bar, a repair shop, a clinic with a back room | A public face that covers for you. Once per case, when one hero's Home clock would tick, it doesn't. |
| Watchtower | A clock tower, a water tank, a penthouse nobody rents | It looks out over a neighborhood. Once per Night, Edge on one lead roll there. |

**Rooms.** One pick each. A room works for every hero on the team.

| Room | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Crime lab | The Crime lab Asset, under your own roof. |
| Med bay | The Back-alley clinic Asset, with no surgeon to pay. |
| Garage | Needs a Vehicle. A wrecked Vehicle is fixed by the next Night, with no Dawn spent. Once per case, the Vehicle starts a chase with one hit already scored. |
| Workshop | At each Dawn there, one hero swaps one piece of gear for another of the same cost, and one smashed piece of gear is repaired. |
| Monitor room | Screens, scanners, a map full of pins. Once per Night, the GM tells the team where the next Scheme beat will land. Nobody ambushes the team in the Lair. |
| Holding cell | Hold one captured named enemy between Nights. Interrogating them there has Edge. Someone will come looking for them. |

A team with no Lair can still take the Crime lab or the Back-alley clinic as a place of its own somewhere in the city.

**Keeping it secret.** No dice roll finds the Lair. The GM uses a move and says the threat first: a tail the heroes didn't shake, a prisoner who saw the way in, a tracker on the Vehicle. If the heroes don't deal with it, the Lair is found. The GM can also choose it as a hero's Home crisis, as the Mastermind's move when the Endgame clock reaches 4, or as part of a task force Crackdown.

**When it is found.** Every hero who uses the Lair ticks Home by 2. From then on the villain can strike there, as a Scheme beat or a Patrol move.

**Defending it.** This is a Confrontation on the heroes' ground. The players draw the map and choose every zone tag. Each room is a zone, and the GM should let what is in it matter: the Workshop is full of tools, and the Garage has a car in it. The enemy picks the moment, so they ambush, unless the Lair has a Monitor room. The enemy comes with an Objective of their own: free a prisoner, take the evidence, burn the place down.

**Losing it.** If the heroes lose that fight, or walk away from a found Lair, it is burned: gone for the rest of the case, rooms and all. When the case closes, the team sets up somewhere new and keeps its rooms. A Lair that was found and held stays found until the team moves it, which costs each hero one Dawn pick.

### Powers

Powers are adapted from the Supersworn: Street Level cards. Each power has three abilities. You start with the first. The second and third are marked *(locked)*: they're locked at creation, and unlocking them is an Advancement option.

**Core powers and more powers.** Twelve powers are **core**: six street and six strange. They are the ones the game is built and tested around. Every other power is on a "More powers" list and is a trial rule: it works the same way, but it has seen less play, so pick one with your GM's agreement.

- **Street powers** are within peak-human reach: training, technology, or a small gift. Write in a **source**.
- **Strange powers** are clearly superhuman. They cost 2 slots. Write in a **source**, as with any power. The flaw you pick for it (Allergy, Unstable or Marked, below) is its price, and the GM can use it as a hook.
- **Slots:** a street power costs 1 slot and a strange power costs 2 (see the slot table above). Advancement adds slots.

**Push (any power):** when your power helps a roll, take 1 Stress to roll with Advantage. Some abilities also cost Stress directly.

**Strange powers cost Stress**: the first time you use a strange power in a fight or scene, take 1 Stress. Bending reality wears on you. Pushing it costs the usual 1 Stress on top. Street powers have no use cost.

**Allergy, Unstable or Marked**: whenever you take a strange power, at creation or later, pick one flaw for it.

- **Allergy:** name a weakness (iron, salt, bright light, a sound). When it's present, the power is at Disadvantage. An always-on power that never rolls (Armored, Invulnerability, Healing Factor) simply doesn't work against it: an Invulnerable hero allergic to electricity takes Harm from a live wire, and Healing Factor won't heal damage it caused. Villains who learn it can use it against you.
- **Unstable:** on a Miss with the power, the GM makes a move and the power also misfires in a way that fits it: a teleport lands in the wrong zone, a fire blast catches an ally, telepathy drags you into someone's nightmare. The GM picks the misfire, and most should hit the scene rather than the team. When one does catch an ally, it's an Impact 1 hit on them (armor and Toughness apply as usual).
- **Marked:** the power shows on your body and you look like a monster. Being seen by civilians ticks Heat by 1, and Presence rolls with strangers are at Disadvantage until they get to know you.

**Always-on powers**: Armored and Invulnerability are strange powers that are always on. Since they're always working, take the 1 Stress the first time the power stops a hit in a fight. They must take Allergy (Unstable has no roll to trigger): while the weakness is present, their Toughness doesn't apply.

### Street powers

These six are the core street powers: the ones the game is built and tested around.

**Gadgeteer** (Technology)

- *Start:* Utility belt. You get 2 belt slots: each Night, fill them with tools or light armor (any one-slot gear that isn't a weapon). A Specialist, who already has a belt, gets 5 belt slots instead of 3.
- *Built for this (locked):* once per Night, reveal a gadget made for exactly this. The roll it helps is an automatic Impact 2.
- *Tinker (locked):* when you Patch Up at Dawn, also repair one broken piece of gear.

**Resilience** (street power; hard to hurt)

- *Start:* Tough. Add one Harm box (Battered, between Hurt and Wounded), and you have Toughness 1 against fists and blunt weapons (not blades or bullets: it is grit, not magic). This doesn't stack with Toughness from Force +3 or from any other source: use your single highest.
- *I can take it (locked):* when you take Harm and stay on your feet, gain 1 Drive (once per round).
- *Won't stay down (locked):* once per Night, when you would go Down, stay at Wounded instead.

**Peak Strength** (peak human; formerly Super-Strength)

- *Start:* Strong like an ox. Edge on Force rolls to lift, break, hold, or throw, and your bare hands count as light melee.
- *Hurl (locked):* on a Strike hit, throw the target into an adjacent zone. If they land on another enemy, both take 1 Harm.
- *Hold the line (locked):* when you Interpose or shield someone, you get +1 Toughness against that hit.

**Enhanced Senses** (loud noise, bright light, or strong smells can overwhelm you)

- *Start:* Keen. Edge on Patrol rolls your sense helps, and Dark zones don't give you Disadvantage.
- *Nothing gets past you (locked):* you can't be ambushed, and Dark doesn't hide anyone from you.
- *On the trail (locked):* when someone gets away, roll Focus against 13. On a hit, you know where they went. On Impact 2+, also take a clue.

**Danger Sense**

- *Start:* Uh oh. +1 Guard. Write the +1 into the Guard number on your sheet.
- *See it coming (locked):* when an attack hits you, take 1 Stress to make the attacker reroll.
- *See the future (locked):* when you miss, the GM offers two moves and you pick which happens.

**Martial Artist** (training)

- *Start:* Trained hands. Your bare hands count as light melee (+1 Harm on a Strike at Impact 2 or better, and Jabs have no -2 penalty). You can't be disarmed, and you have Edge on rolls to grab or disarm.
- *Turn it back (locked):* once per round, when an enemy you're engaged with misses you, make a free Jab against them.
- *Pressure point (locked):* on a Strike hit at Impact 2 or better, choose one: the target loses their free move, or has Disadvantage on their next attack.

**Custom Power** (write in name and source)

- *Start:* Edge on rolls outside a fight that your power helps.
- *Second ability (locked):* Edge on rolls in a fight that your power helps.
- *Third ability (locked):* write your own with the GM, matching the others in strength.

### More street powers (trial)

Not yet tested as much as the core six. Pick one if your GM agrees.

**Fast Healer** (tough and quick to recover; formerly Healing Factor)

- *Start:* Fast mender. A breather clears Bruised and Hurt.
- *Shake it off (locked):* once per fight, Quick Action: clear one Harm box.
- *Not today (locked):* the first time you would die, you survive, out of action until the next Night. Then cross this off.

**Special Weapon** (write in the weapon)

- *Start:* That ain't a knife. Your weapon works like the closest ordinary weapon (light, heavy or gun) and replaces your free weapon. Your first attack each fight with it has Edge.
- *How's it feel? (locked):* on a hit with it, choose one: the target takes 1 Stress, or +1 Harm on Impact 2 or better.
- *This is a knife (locked):* once per fight, take 1 Stress to reroll a missed attack with it.

**Quick** (fast for a human; formerly Super-Speed)

- *Start:* Fast. Your free move covers two zones.
- *Watch me (locked):* Edge on Flow rolls in a fight.
- *Burst (locked):* once per turn, take 1 Stress for an extra Quick Action with Advantage.

**Tendrils** (Technology)

- *Start:* Movement. Climbing and swinging never need a roll, and your free move can reach High Ground.
- *Combat (locked):* when your Jab hits, you can grab the target instead of hurting them: they can't move on their next turn.
- *Multi-tasking (locked):* once per turn, take 1 Stress to do two Quick Actions with one roll.

**Electrical Aptitude** (Technology). You hold **Charge**, 0 to 3.

- *Start:* Spark. You start every fight with 1 Charge, and touching live power is a Quick Action to regain it (max 1). Spend 1 Charge for Edge on a roll electricity helps.
- *Surge (locked):* grab high voltage: roll Force against 13. On a hit, gain 3 Charge (max 3). On a miss, take 2 Harm.
- *Discharge (locked):* spend up to 2 Charge on an attack: +1 Harm per Charge spent, on a hit.

**Reputation** (write in what the street says about you)

- *Start:* Known. Edge on Presence rolls outside a fight with anyone who has heard the stories. In your own neighborhood that is nearly everyone.
- *They hesitate (locked):* once per fight, when a mook group is about to attack you, roll Presence against their Poise. On a hit, they lose that attack.
- *Say the word (locked):* once per Night, one mook group with no named enemy in sight runs or gives up, no roll.

### Strange powers

These six are the core strange powers.

**Shadow Manipulation**

- *Start:* You can Vanish in any zone with shadows. While Hidden in a Dark zone, your attacks get Edge on top of Advantage.
- *Spread the dark (locked):* Quick Action: roll Focus against 13. On a hit your zone becomes Dark, and adjacent zones too on Impact 2+.
- *Reveal (locked):* once per fight, when you drop out of Hidden for dramatic effect, gain 2 Drive.

**Invulnerability** (your Allergy is the weakness)

- *Start:* when you would take Harm from a physical attack, take that much Stress instead. Work out the Harm first (weapon bonuses, then Toughness, then the 1-Harm minimum), then convert what's left. Your weakness gets through normally, and once your Stress is full (8), anything more spills into Harm. Since every hit already costs Stress, Invulnerability skips the always-on first-hit Stress cost.
- *Untouchable (locked):* after people see you shrug off a hit, Edge on Presence rolls to intimidate them.
- *Can't die (locked):* if you would die, even to your weakness, you fall into a coma for 2d10 days instead.

**Mighty** (beyond peak human; the strange version of Peak Strength)

- *Start:* lift and throw cars. Force attacks get +1 Harm on Impact 2+ and ignore Toughness, and you can throw heavy objects at Near range.
- *Wrecking (locked):* Full Action: smash a wall, floor or vehicle to remove Cover from a zone, open a path between zones, or make a zone Hazard.
- *Big leap (locked):* your free move can cross two zones or reach High Ground.

**Healing Factor** (wounds close as you watch; the strange version of Fast Healer)

- *Start:* at the end of each of your turns in a fight, clear one Harm box (not Down). If anyone hurt you since your last turn, that heal costs 1 Stress. The first time it heals you in a fight, take the usual 1 Stress too.
- *Get back up (locked):* when you go Down, you're back up at Wounded at the end of your next turn, no Drive needed.
- *Won't die (locked):* when you would die, you don't. You're out until the next scene, then up at Wounded. Your Allergy (a rare alloy, fire, a beheading blade) can still kill you.

**Telepathy** (-1 to each roll for each extra person you target)

- *Start:* Quick Action: roll Focus against a target's Wits. On a hit, you read their surface thoughts, and your next roll against them has Edge (Advantage on Impact 2+).
- *Influence (locked):* take 1 Stress, roll Focus against Poise. On a hit, they do one reasonable thing you suggest.
- *Control (locked):* take 2 Stress, roll Focus against Poise. On a hit, you control one person for a scene. On Impact 1 it's only a few minutes, and they remember. You can't take over a named enemy during a fight: against one, Control works as Influence. Burnout: on a Miss, take 2 more Stress, Telepathy shuts off for the rest of the scene, and the target knows exactly what you tried.

**Fear Aura**

- *Start:* Edge on Presence rolls to intimidate.
- *Dread (locked):* take 1 Stress: a Presence attack against every mook group in your zone. Any group you beat runs. Fearless mooks (fanatics, cultists, the mind-controlled) ignore Dread.
- *Nightmare (locked):* take 2 Stress: a Presence attack against one named enemy's Poise. On a hit, they take Stress equal to the Impact and can't attack you on their next turn (see Named enemies resist being shut down).

### More strange powers (trial)

Not yet tested as much as the core six. Pick one if your GM agrees.

**Teleportation**

- *Start:* Quick Action: move to any zone you can see. No roll, unless you're escaping someone engaged with you or the zone is Dark, Crowd or smoke-filled: then roll Focus vs 12. On a Miss you land somewhere bad (GM's choice).
- *Combat (locked):* after you teleport, your next attack that turn has Edge.
- *Long distance (locked):* take 3 Stress to go anywhere in the city you've been before.

**Elementalist** (wind, water, earth, or fire)

- *Start:* Manipulate. When your element is in the zone, Edge on rolls that use it.
- *Unleash (locked):* Full Action. Either attack with your element at Near range, with Edge, or reshape a Near zone until the end of the fight: earth adds Cover, water adds Hazard, wind adds Dark or clears smoke and Dark.
- *Draw (locked):* take 1 Stress to pull your element from miles away, so the other abilities work anywhere.

**Kinesis** (move things with your mind; the street-scale version of Telekinesis)

- *Start:* Push, pull, or lift things your size or smaller. Edge on rolls it helps.
- *Desperate (locked):* after a roll, take 2 Stress to add +2.
- *Great feat (locked):* at 6 Drive, spend all of it: one roll your power fuels is an automatic Impact 3.

**Armored**

- *Start:* Toughness 1 against everything.
- *Upgrade (locked):* Toughness 2 against guns and blades.
- *Let it take the hit (locked):* once per fight, ignore one hit entirely. Roll a d10: on 1 to 3, the armor breaks until you Patch Up at Dawn.

**Animalism** (pick a species or type)

- *Start:* You can talk to them, and Presence works on them like people.
- *Call (locked):* Full Action, take 1 Stress: a group of 3 animals arrives and acts as an allied mook group for the scene. Allied mooks act on your turn and use one of your Quick Actions.
- *Swarm (locked):* as Call, but a group of 6.

**Shapeshifter** (pick an animal)

- *Start:* Full Action, take 1 Stress: become the animal for a scene. +1 to one stat the form helps, -1 Focus. On a miss while shifted, the animal takes over until the scene ends. Shapeshifter always takes Allergy (silver, wolfsbane, a sound), since losing control is already built in.
- *Settled (locked):* you can stay shifted as long as you like, and only lose control on a miss with doubles.
- *Hybrid (locked):* take 2 Stress: for one fight, Advantage on Force and intimidation. Any miss and you attack the nearest person.

**Vampirism** (you must feed on people: blood, psychic energy, or emotions). You hold **Energy**, 0 to 5.

- *Start:* Feed. Roll Presence against a victim's Poise to gain Energy: +3 on Impact 2+, +2 on Impact 1, +2 and 1 Stress on a miss. Heat from feeding: none on Impact 2+ unless someone saw you; +1 Heat on Impact 1 (a witness, or a body that gets found); +1 Heat on a miss. Spend Energy for +1 per point on a roll, up to +2 per roll. At 0 Energy, take 1 Harm each Dawn until you feed.
- *Glamour (locked):* spend 1 Energy for Advantage on a Presence roll.
- *Shapeshift (locked):* spend 2 Energy to become a wolf or bat for a scene, Edge on rolls the form helps.

**Elasticity**

- *Start:* Squeeze. You fit through any gap. Edge on escapes and getting past barriers.
- *Stretch (locked):* your Strikes reach Near range, and can hit two enemies in one zone with one roll.
- *Bounce (locked):* Toughness 1 against blunt hits and falls. Follows the always-on rule: take 1 Stress the first time it stops a hit in a fight.

**Illusions**

- *Start:* Create an illusion that fools one sense. Edge on deception rolls and Vanish.
- *Full illusion (locked):* take 1 Stress: an illusion that fools every sense. Once per scene, your next deception or escape roll is an automatic Impact 2.
- *Perfect illusion (locked):* take 2 Stress: everyone who sees it believes it. Once per scene, your next deception or escape roll is an automatic Impact 3 (never an attack).

**Force Fields**

- *Start:* Quick Action: roll Focus against 13. On a hit, Toughness 2 until your next turn (Toughness 1 on Impact 1).
- *Shared (locked):* you can cover an ally in your zone instead.
- *Containment (locked):* Full Action: roll Focus against a target's Guard. On a hit, they're trapped while you keep focusing (you can't attack). Each of their turns, they can roll Force against your Wits to break free. A named enemy rolls with Edge, and the hold ends after they have been held for one full round. It counts as their one lost turn: see Named enemies resist being shut down.

**Temporal Instability** (formerly Time Travel; always takes Unstable: misfires slip you or an ally out of sync with time)

- *Start:* Look. Take 1 Stress to see a place's past. On a lead roll hit there, take an extra clue.
- *Redo (locked):* take 2 Stress after a miss to redo the roll with Edge.
- *Loop (locked):* take 3 Stress to reroll Crack the Case.

**Ice Control**

- *Start:* Edge when you use ice to block, climb, or slide. You can make your zone a Hazard (ice) as a Quick Action.
- *Frost (locked):* ice attacks at Near range, with Edge.
- *Freeze (locked):* take 3 Stress: every mook in your zone loses their next turn. Named villains there resist: roll Focus against each one's Guard, and only those you beat lose their turn (see Named enemies resist being shut down).

**Weather Control**

- *Start:* Change the weather over a neighborhood in about ten minutes. Rain, fog, or storm can make zones Dark or Hazard.
- *Drastic (locked):* change it in a round, even impossibly (snow in July, rain indoors). Each use ticks Heat by 1: a freak storm is obviously somebody's doing.
- *Weather as a weapon (locked):* attacks with lightning, hail, or wind at Near or Far range, with Edge.

*Conspicuous:* there's no subtle way to use Weather Control. Your eyes glow, the wind circles you, the clouds center on you. Using any of its abilities ends Hidden, you can't Vanish that round, and anyone watching knows it's you.

**Cloning**

- *Start:* One clone. When it Helps you, you get Advantage instead of Edge. Once per fight it can take a hit for you, then it's gone until Dawn.
- *Many (locked):* your clones act as an allied mook group of 3. Like all allied mooks, they act on your turn and use one of your Quick Actions.
- *The Prestige (locked):* one perfect clone. If you ever die, keep playing as the clone, but it comes back a little wrong: start at 4 Stress (Fraying) and tick Home by 2.

**Plant Control**

- *Start:* Edge on rolls where plants help (needs plants in the zone).
- *Entangle (locked):* when your attack hits, the target can't move on their next turn instead of taking Harm.
- *Green healing (locked):* a breather among plants clears Bruised and Hurt.

**Sand Manipulation**

- *Start:* Turn part or all of yourself to sand. Edge on escapes, squeezing, and slipping grabs.
- *Sand fists (locked):* your bare hands count as heavy melee (+1 Harm on any hit) and work with Strikes, not Jabs.
- *Reform (locked):* Patch Up anywhere with sand clears Down too. If you would die, you scatter and reform in 2d10 days. Water is the natural Allergy for this power.

**Sorcery** (spells, rituals, old books)

- *Start:* at the start of each Night, prepare 3 spells. Casting one is a Quick Action, once per spell per fight (roll Focus where a roll is needed). Bolt: a Near attack that ignores Toughness. Ward: +2 Guard to an ally until your next turn. Bind: on a hit, the target can't move on their next turn. Sight: see the hidden, invisible or supernatural in your zone.
- *Deeper study (locked):* prepare 4 spells.
- *Ritual (locked):* out of a fight, take 3 Stress and spend a scene: scry for a clue about a person or place, or call up help for one fight.

**Luck** (probability bends around you)

- *Start:* once per fight, reroll any roll you make and keep the new result.
- *Share it (locked):* you can give that reroll to an ally instead.
- *Lucky break (locked):* once per Night, turn one of your Misses into Impact 1.

If Luck takes Unstable, its misfire is bad luck spilling over: on your Miss, an ally nearby takes the GM's move instead of you.

**Iron Skin**

- *Start:* Quick Action to turn your skin to metal, paying the usual 1 Stress for a strange power. It lasts until the end of the fight or scene. While armored: Toughness 1 against everything, including guns, but you're heavy and slow, with Disadvantage on Vanish and climbing, and you can't use Flow to dodge falls or swim. Your metal fists count as heavy melee (+1 Harm on any hit, Full Action Strikes).
- *Steel (locked):* once per fight while armored, take 1 Stress to reduce the Harm of one hit by 1 more, to a minimum of 0.
- *Unbreakable (locked):* once per Night, when a hit would put you Down, take 2 Stress instead of the Harm.

**Shock Touch**

- *Start:* when your Jab hits, the target loses their next Quick Action (a named enemy has Disadvantage on their next roll instead: see Named enemies resist being shut down).
- *Blackout (locked):* a Strike that also kills the lights. Your zone becomes Dark.
- *Overload (locked):* take 1 Stress: on a hit with your Strike, the target loses their whole next turn (see Named enemies resist being shut down) and one gun or gadget they carry is fried. (Like all electricity, Shock Touch already ignores Toughness.)

**Levitation** (a light, slow cousin of Flight)

- *Start:* Float. You pay the usual 1 Stress the first time you use it in a fight or scene. After that, hovering is free. Your free move can reach High Ground or drop from any height, and falls never hurt you.
- *Drift (locked):* Quick Action: rise out of reach. Until the start of your next turn, enemies on the ground can only reach you with ranged attacks, and your free move can cross one extra zone through open air. The first round is free. Each round after that costs 1 Stress.
- *Lift (locked):* carry one person your size when you float. The first time each scene, that costs 1 Stress.

**Blast** (pick one energy: fire, force, light, sound, lightning)

- *Start:* a ranged attack with Focus at Near or Far, with no Far penalty. +1 Harm on Impact 2 or better, and it ignores Toughness. Like a gun, it is at Disadvantage when you're engaged in melee. Nobody can take it from you, but it is loud and bright: +1 Heat for each fight where you use it in public.
- *Wide beam (locked):* take 1 Stress: one attack roll against every enemy in one zone you can see. Allies in that zone are caught too.
- *Overcharge (locked):* take 2 Stress before you roll: on a hit, +2 Harm instead of +1, and the target is knocked into an adjacent zone.

**Custom Strange Power** (write in name and source)

- *Start:* Edge on rolls your power helps, in or out of a fight. It pays the usual 1 Stress and takes a flaw, like any strange power.
- *Second ability (locked):* write your own with the GM, matching the second ability of a listed strange power.
- *Third ability (locked):* write your own with the GM, matching the third.

### Villain-only powers

Too big for street-level heroes, but fine for a Nemesis: **Biokinesis** (reshape your body, slip away as a puddle), **Invisibility** (always Hidden unless you attack), **Flight** (move to any zone, ignore High Ground), **Telekinesis** (throw things at any range, lift a truck for 2 Stress), and **Clairvoyance** (prophetic dreams; the GM can call one villain move "foreseen" and it can't be stopped by Called It). ## Villains

A villain fits in a short stat block. They use the same stats, defenses, and Drive prices as heroes, but skip the parts that only matter for player characters.

### What a villain has

- **Stats and defenses**, same as a hero. As a guide (trial), a case villain's stats total 7 or 8, and a lieutenant's total 4 to 7.
- **Harm:** 5 for a lieutenant, 10 for a case's main villain (8 for a smaller one). A case villain who fights alone, with no mooks and no other named enemy, has 2 more Harm: 12, or 10 for a smaller one (trial).
- **Stress:** a lieutenant has 4 boxes (Fraying at 2, Breaking at 4). A case villain has 6 (Fraying at 3, Breaking at 6). Presence attacks and Stress Flairs fill it. A Breaking villain does something reckless: flees, turns on their own people, or gives something away.
- **Trait:** one line for Fraying and one for Breaking. Use a hero's Trait, a villain Trait (below), or write your own.
- **Drive:** starts at 2, earned from their role trigger once per round. Same spend prices as heroes.
- **Trick:** one special move that costs Drive.
- **Weakness:** something the heroes can discover (a Detective's Read, an Impact 3 on Patrol, Crack the Case or Unmasking) and exploit.

**Using a Weakness (trial).** A Weakness is a promise from the GM. When the heroes play into one they know, it works: the villain takes the challenge, goes for the ledger, lets the hostage go. No roll is needed to make them bite. If a roll does come of it, the first one each scene has Edge.

**A villain who Breaks (trial)** doesn't leave the fight. Their Breaking line takes over for the rest of the scene, and every attack against them has Edge. If the Breaking line says they flee, the heroes get one round to stop them.

**Answer Toughness:** every named villain should have at least one way past Toughness: fire, electricity, gas, a hazard they can trigger, or a Presence attack. Otherwise an armored hero can't be hurt.

**Scaling to the party:**

- **Villain turns:** a named villain gets one turn per round for every two heroes, rounded up. One turn against 1 or 2 heroes, two against 3 or 4. Spread the turns through the round. A villain fighting alone, with no mooks or lieutenants, gets one turn per hero instead, and 2 more Harm (trial).
- **Lieutenants and several named enemies:** a lieutenant gets one turn and one free Presence attack per round, however many heroes there are, even fighting alone. When a fight has more than one named enemy, each villain takes its own turns by the rule above and each lieutenant takes one; no villain counts as alone while another named enemy is in the fight. Spread all their turns through the round.
- **Gang size:** in a fight with no named villain, start with one group of 3 mooks per hero. With a named villain present, one group per two heroes (rounded down, minimum 1). Then adjust for how hard the fight should be. For a harder fight, make one mook group bruisers, or all of them (trial). Don't add extra groups on top when a named villain is in the fight: that is a much bigger jump.

**Accomplices.** A named underling who isn't a lieutenant (a corrupt inspector, a fixer) is an **accomplice**. In Arcs, Pawn keeps its own meaning: the villain of a case, working for the Mastermind.

### Named enemies resist being shut down (trial)

A named enemy (a villain or a lieutenant) can lose at most one turn per fight, from all sources combined. After that, any effect that would cost them a turn gives them Disadvantage on their next roll instead. "Loses their next Quick Action" against a named enemy always means Disadvantage on their next roll. "Disadvantage on their next roll" applies to their next roll that isn't their free Presence attack. Mooks lose turns as written.

### Roles

| --- | --- | --- |
| Heavy | They deal or take Impact 2+ | Rampage (2 Drive): right after taking a hit, make a free Strike at whoever dealt it, if they can reach them |
| Schemer | One of their clocks ticks | A mook group acts out of turn (1), tick a villain clock (2) |
| Nemesis | They target their rival | Knows your moves (2 Drive): when the rival spends Drive, the Nemesis has Edge against them until the end of the round |

Lever (2 Drive): name one of a hero's connections; that hero takes 1 Stress, or has Disadvantage until the end of their next turn |
| Mooks | Never | None. They act in groups of 3. |

### Villain Traits (trial)

Heroes' Traits work for villains too (the Butcher is Spite, Voss is Order). These ten are for villains only. Like every villain Trait, each is two lines: what they do when Fraying, and what they do when they Break. Fraying should give the heroes an opening; Breaking should be reckless.

| Trait | Who has it | Fraying | Breaking |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Greed** | Wants more, whatever it costs other people. | Protects the money before their people. A hero who threatens the stash or offers a cut has Edge against them. | Grabs what they can carry and runs, leaving their crew to take the fall. |
| **Desperation** | Has run out of other options: a debt, a deadline, someone held over them. | Takes any risk to finish the job. Their attacks have Edge, and so do attacks against them. | Does the one thing they swore they never would: hurts a hostage, lights the fuse, or begs. |
| **Narcissism** | Needs to be admired, and can't stand being laughed at. | Has to be seen winning. Won't take cover or back off while anyone is watching, and goes after whoever mocks them. | Drops the plan to humiliate whoever humiliated them, in front of everyone. |
| **Lust** | Wants someone (or wants to own them), and won't take no. | Fixates on the one they want. Won't harm them, and goes after anyone standing between them. | Tries to take that person and get out with them. The heroes get one round to stop it. |
| **Viciousness** | Enjoys the hurting. | Goes after the most hurt target in reach, hero or bystander, instead of the biggest threat. | Hurts whoever is closest and can't fight back, their own people included, until someone stops them. |
| **Paranoia** | Sure that everyone is out to get them, and sometimes right. | Sees a trap in everything. Keeps a mook group between them and the heroes, and any bluff about backup, cops or a traitor has Edge against them. | Turns on their own lieutenant or bolts for the nearest exit, certain it's a setup. |
| **Envy** | Wants what someone else has: the fame, the family, the life. | Goes after the hero who has what they lack and ignores the rest. | Leaves the fight to wreck something that hero loves. The heroes get one round to stop them. |
| **Contempt** | Thinks the heroes, and most people, are beneath them. | Won't take the heroes seriously. Toys with the weakest-looking hero instead of the biggest threat. | Drops the act and throws everything at whoever got under their skin, plan and people be damned. |
| **Zealotry** | Believes in a cause, and that the cause makes them right. | Can't be talked down or bought: Presence attacks against them have Disadvantage. Won't retreat, either. | Tries to take everyone down with them for the cause: themselves, their followers, the building. |
| **Control** | Has to run everything and everyone. | Everything has to go to plan. When a hero changes the fight (a door, the lights, a zone tag), they spend their next turn putting it back. | Destroys what they can't control any more: the evidence, the building, the hostage. The heroes get one round to stop it. |

## Old Habits: changing your Trait

People change, but not cleanly. At the end of an **arc** (several cases tied together), a hero can trade their Trait for a new one, if the player and GM agree the story earned it.

### The first change

Take the new Trait. The old one becomes an **Old Habit**: you can still use its Steady perk, but it costs 1 Drive each time. Its hook and Breaking fallout are gone.

Then pick **2** of these:

- Clear all Stress and reset your Home clock to 0.
- Gain a Flair that fits the new Trait.
- Your maximum Drive goes up to 7.

### The second change

This should be rare, maybe once in a long campaign. Take the new Trait and lose your Old Habit entirely. The table picks **2** kinds of fallout:

- **Allies shift:** a contact, team member, or faction sees you differently.
- **Public opinion:** start a new clock for how much the city trusts (or fears) you.
- **The nemesis adapts:** a recurring villain gains a new Trick built to counter the new you.
- **The city remembers:** add a lasting change to the setting that reflects who you became.
- **The price of becoming someone new:** give up a resource, a relationship, or your home turf.

## Arcs

An arc is 3 to 5 cases tied together by someone working behind the scenes, the **Mastermind**. Each case stands on its own, but each one leaves fingerprints that point at the same person.

The threat behind an arc isn't always a person. See Arcs without a Mastermind, at the end of this chapter.

### The arc sheet (GM)

- **Mastermind:** who's really behind it. This can start blank (see Promotion).
- **Endgame:** what happens to the city if they win.
- **Pawns:** the villains of each case. Each one works for the Mastermind, knowingly or not, and each knows one thing about them.
- **Calling card:** one detail that turns up in every case: a shell company's name, a chalk mark, a matchbook. Players should spot it by the second case.
- **Endgame clock:** 8 segments.
- **Arc clues:** a running list, separate from case clues.

### The Endgame clock

| It ticks when | Ticks |
| --- | --- |
| A case's Scheme clock fills | +2 |
| A Pawn escapes, or a theory names the wrong culprit | +1 |
| The heroes miss an Unmasking roll | +1 |

- **At 4:** the Mastermind makes it personal. Pick one hero and hit their Home: a loved one is threatened, a landlord calls, someone starts asking about their nights.
- **At 8:** the Endgame happens. The finale becomes damage control instead of prevention.

### Getting arc clues

- Impact 3 on Crack the Case (already in the rules).
- Impact 3 on a lead: take an arc clue as your bonus instead.
- Finding the calling card in a case, the first time each case.
- Taking a Pawn alive and breaking them in an interrogation scene (Presence against their Poise).

### Promotion: when the GM hasn't decided yet

An arc can start **Floating**, the same way a case can. The arc sheet just says *someone*. As cases play out, watch for the villain who got away, the one the players hate most, the name that keeps coming up. When the GM is ready (usually around the third case, or when the Endgame clock hits 4), promote that character to Mastermind and fill in the sheet. Reread the old arc clues in that light. Any that don't fit were someone else's mess. The players never need to know when it happened.

### Unmasking

With at least 3 arc clues, the team can theorize the arc: who the Mastermind is and what their Endgame is. Roll **2d10 + arc clues against 16** (Complexity 6). With 4 arc clues that's right 45% of the time, 5 arc clues gets you to 55%, and 6 arc clues to 64%.

| Result | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Miss | Wrong, and the Mastermind knows you're onto them. Endgame clock +1. |
| Impact 1 | Right, but they're a step ahead. The finale happens on their terms. |
| Impact 2 | Right. You choose when and where the finale happens. |
| Impact 3 | Right, plus their Weakness, and one of their Pawns turns on them |

If the roll hits but names the wrong person, you've found the Mastermind's right hand, as with cases.

### The finale

The finale is a case against the Mastermind at finale Complexity (2 more than a normal case for your team). Afterwards, each hero can change their Trait (Old Habits). A beaten Mastermind can come back in a later arc as a Pawn, or as someone's Nemesis.

### Example arc: The Landlord

- **Mastermind:** Augustine Voss, a landlord quietly buying up the east side.
- **Endgame:** empty the neighborhood, burn what's left, and rebuild it as his.
- **Pawns:** the Butcher (a protection racket that scares tenants out), an arson crew, a crooked fire inspector, a city councilman.
- **Calling card:** every building that gets hit has an eviction notice from the same shell company, Harbor & Main Holdings.
- **Scheme beats (first case):** at 1, a tenant who talked to the heroes disappears. At 3, the Calloway annex burns. At 5, the city condemns the block. At 6, Voss pulls it off: the tenants are evicted the morning after the inspection.

### Arcs without a Mastermind (trial)

Some arcs have nobody behind them: a river rising, a rift opening, a drug spreading, a gang war nobody planned. Run them with the same sheet and the same clock. Only the names change.

| With a Mastermind | Without one |
| --- | --- |
| **Mastermind:** who is behind it | **Threat:** what is coming, and why nobody has stopped it |
| **Pawns:** each case's villain works for the Mastermind | **Symptoms:** each case's villain is caused by the Threat, profits from it, or is fighting it the wrong way. Each knows one thing about it |
| **Calling card:** one detail in every case | **Sign:** one detail in every case. Ash on the windowsills, the same dream, water where it shouldn't be |
| **Unmasking:** who they are and what they want | **Understanding:** what it is and what stops it |

**The Endgame clock** has 8 segments and ticks the same way: +2 when a case's Scheme clock fills, +1 when a Symptom is left unsolved (its villain escapes, or a theory names the wrong culprit), and +1 on a missed Understanding roll. At 4, the Threat reaches a hero's neighborhood. At 8, it happens, and the finale is damage control.

**Understanding.** With at least 3 arc clues, the team says what the Threat is and what will stop it. Roll 2d10 + arc clues against 16, as for Unmasking.

| Result | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Miss | Wrong, and the wrong cure makes it worse. Endgame clock +1. |
| Impact 1 | Right, but the answer has a price: one step of the finale will cost a hero something they care about. |
| Impact 2 | Right. You choose when and where to make your stand. |
| Impact 3 | Right, and you find its weak point: one step of the finale's Objective is already done. |

**The finale** is won by an Objective, not a knockout: 4 to 6 steps (close the rift, open the floodgates, get the block out, burn the lab). Give it a face all the same, so there is someone to fight: a cult that wants it, a profiteer, a thing it sent ahead, the one person who could have stopped it and didn't. Dropping them helps. Only the Objective ends it.

**A Threat can grow a face.** If the players decide someone must be behind it and their answer is better than yours, promote that person to Mastermind and carry on (see Promotion).

Three to start from:

- **The Flood.** *Threat:* the river is rising and the levee money was stolen years ago. *Endgame:* the low neighborhoods go under. *Symptoms:* looters, a contractor burying evidence, a landlord locking tenants in to save the insurance, a crew sandbagging only their own block. *Sign:* water where it shouldn't be.
- **The Rift.** *Threat:* an alignment three weeks off will open something under the old subway. *Endgame:* it opens, and it stays open. *Symptoms:* people with new powers and no control, a cult trying to hurry it, a scientist trying to bottle it, a thing that already slipped through. *Sign:* everyone near it has the same dream.
- **The War.** *Threat:* two gangs sliding into open war that neither boss wants and neither can stop. *Endgame:* the east side becomes a battlefield and the task force moves in for good. *Symptoms:* a revenge killing, a gun shipment, a kidnapped peacemaker, a cop selling to both sides. *Sign:* the same batch of stolen rifles.

## Test fight: The Cannery

One short fight to check the math: **Rook** (Brawler) and **Nightjar** (Stalker) against **the Butcher** and 6 Fifth Street Saints, with a hostage on the line. It should take 4 to 6 rounds.

&#91;embedded content: The Cannery · 4 zones\]

### Setup

- The heroes cracked the case with an Impact 2, so they chose the ground: Nightjar starts Hidden on the Catwalk.
- Both heroes start with 2 Drive, no Harm, and no Stress.
- **Hazard (Kill Floor):** anyone who misses a roll in this zone takes 1 Harm from the hooks. A Haymaker can knock someone into them.
- **Hostage clock: 4.** It ticks at the end of every round, after everyone's turns (clocks in a fight always tick last). When it fills, the Butcher's men hurt the hostage, and every hero takes 2 Stress.

### Winning

The heroes win if they drop the Butcher, **or** they finish the Objective, **Free the hostage** (3 steps, all in the Freezer, and no step can be taken while an enemy is in that zone):

1. **Break the padlock:** Force or Focus against rating -1 (a chained door). *Pays:* the hostage clock does not tick this round.
2. **Cut the hostage loose:** Flow or Focus against rating +1. *Pays:* the hostage clock stops for good. The Butcher can still use Human shield if he is in the Freezer.
3. **Get the hostage out the back:** Flow or Presence against rating +1 (move them, or talk them through it). *Pays:* the Objective is done.

The Saints run when 3 of them are down, unless the Butcher can see them.

### What to watch for

- How many rounds did it take? The target is 4 to 6.
- Did anyone go Down? Did Rook's Toughness make a difference?
- How much Drive did each hero earn and spend? Did anyone get to a Signature Move?
- Did anyone reach Fraying? Did the GM use a hook?
- Was Jab ever worth it over a Strike?
- Did the hostage clock change what the heroes did, or did they ignore it?
- Did anyone choose the Objective over attacking, and was it the faster way to win?
- Did the zones and Hidden feel tactical without counting squares?

After one or two runs, adjust the numbers that felt off: Harm boxes, villain Harm, Drive earn rate, or the Impact bands.
