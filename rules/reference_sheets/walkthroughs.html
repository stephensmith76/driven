Twelve situations played out at the table, roll by roll, to show how the rules fit together. Every roll is 2d10 + stat vs a defense, and the margin sets the Impact: below 0 Miss, 0-3 Impact 1, 4-7 Impact 2, 8+ Impact 3. The dice are written as 7+4 +3 = 14 vs 13: the two dice, then the stat and any Edge, then the defense.

1Pulling a kid out of a burning building

Rules: Objectives, Hazard zones, fire spreading, soft and hard moves, Strain, Drive from steps.

A tenement is on fire and a boy is trapped on the third floor. No enemies, so the fire is the opposition. The GM draws three zones: Street, Stairwell (Hazard: smoke, so it also counts as Dark) and Third Floor (Hazard: fire). The Objective has three steps: force the jammed stairwell door (rating -1), find the boy in the smoke (+1), and carry him down the stairs (+1).

  1. Round 1, Nightjar. She fires her grapple line (Quick Action) and swings in through a third-floor window. With her second Quick Action she kicks open the apartment door (Interact).
  2. Round 1, Bulwark. Free move into the Stairwell, then the first step as a Full Action: Force vs 11. 5+4 +3 = 12 vs 11, margin 1, Impact 1. The door gives, with a cost. The GM offers two: 1 Harm, or the fire clock ticks. Dana takes 1 Harm (Bruised). Finishing a step counts as her archetype trigger: +1 Drive. Running into a fire for a stranger is her Mercy trigger too: +1 more.
  3. Round 2, Nightjar. Searching the smoke: Focus vs 13. 4+3 +2 = 9 vs 13, a Miss. A miss in a Hazard zone costs a flat 1 Harm, no Toughness: Bruised. The GM makes a soft move and says the threat out loud: "The ceiling is sagging. At the end of next round it comes down on this floor."
  4. Round 2, Bulwark. Free move up to the Third Floor. Help (Quick): Nightjar's next roll has Edge. Interact (Quick): she soaks a blanket in the bathtub.
  5. End of round 2. Fire spreads to one adjacent zone unless someone puts it out. The Stairwell is now on fire as well as full of smoke.
  6. Round 3, Nightjar. Second try, with Edge: 6+3 +2 +2 = 13 vs 13, margin 0, Impact 1. She finds him under the bed. Cost: 1 Stress or the fire clock ticks; she takes the Stress. +1 Drive for the step. Then she Strains (1 more Stress) for an extra Quick Action and grapples back out the window before the ceiling falls.
  7. Round 3, Bulwark. Free move into the burning Stairwell with the boy under the wet blanket. The last step: Force vs 13. 8+6 +3 = 17 vs 13, margin 4, Impact 2. Clean. They're out, and finishing the Objective ends the scene. +1 Drive.

What it shows: a scene with no villain still runs in rounds when there's a clock. Hazards punish misses, not presence, so the heroes chose when to risk a roll there. The GM warned about the ceiling a round ahead, so if Nightjar had stayed, it would have been a fair hard move.

2Sneaking into a warehouse

Rules: a lead roll to get in unseen, "you get the clue no matter what", the ambush cost, Vanish and Hidden, Guards' morale, leaving a fight.

Nightjar wants the shipping manifest inside a guarded warehouse on the docks. Getting into a place unseen is a lead roll. The GM rates the warehouse +1 (defense 13).

  1. The lead roll. Over the fence and through a skylight: Flow vs 13. 2+5 +4 = 11 vs 13, a Miss. She still gets the clue: she photographs the manifest. The GM picks the cost from the lead table, an ambush. The lights snap on, and a group of three guards is between her and the door.
  2. The fight starts. The GM draws Office (Dark, where she is) and Loading Floor (Cover). Guards have Wits 13.
  3. Round 1, Nightjar. Vanish (Quick): Flow vs the highest Wits watching. 6+4 +4 = 14 vs 13, a hit. She's Hidden: she can't be targeted, and her first attack from Hidden has Advantage. She saves that attack for next turn instead of wasting it on a Jab.
  4. Round 1, guards. They can't target her. They search: a Quick Action, their attack bonus vs 12 + her Flow. 7+5 +1 = 13 vs 16, a miss. She stays Hidden.
  5. Round 2, Nightjar. A Strike from Hidden with her fighting sticks, with Advantage: she rolls three dice and keeps the best two. 9, 6, (2) +4 = 19 vs 12, margin 7, Impact 2. A guard drops, and attacking ends Hidden. Attacking while Hidden is her Stalker trigger: +1 Drive.
  6. Morale. Guards are paid, not loyal: a group gives up when its first guard drops. The other two raise their hands. But one was standing at the end of round 1, so the alarm went out: backup arrives on round 3.
  7. Round 3, Nightjar. She's in a zone with an exit and nobody is engaged with her, so she spends her turn to leave the fight before the backup's headlights reach the gate.

What it shows: a failed sneak still moves the case forward; the Miss decided what it cost. Hidden is a setup, not an attack: the payoff comes on the next turn.

3Talking past the front desk

Rules: Presence vs Poise outside a fight, spending Drive for Edge, Impact 1 costs, soft moves on a miss.

The Ledger needs ten minutes in the records room of a shipping company. One guard at the desk (Poise 11). Ruth plays a city auditor on a surprise visit.

  1. Before the roll. The player knows a miss here could burn the whole approach, so Ruth spends 1 Drive for Edge (+2). Edge has to be bought before the dice are rolled.
  2. The bluff. Presence vs his Poise: 4+6 +2 +2 = 14 vs 11, margin 3, Impact 1. She's in, with a cost.
  3. The cost. The GM offers two that fit the scene: "he'll remember your face (+1 Heat)" or "he wants to call your office to confirm, so one of your connections gets a phone call". Ruth picks the Heat. Her niece doesn't need to be part of this.
  4. If she had missed. A soft move, said out loud: "He picks up the phone to check with upstairs." Ruth would get a chance to act before it lands, by talking faster, leaving, or walking past him anyway.

What it shows: Impact 1 is a success. The cost is the player's choice of two, so it never feels like a punishment from nowhere. Drive is best spent before the rolls that matter most.

4Forcing your way past the bouncers

Rules: bruisers, weapon bonuses, Pile Through, Toughness, morale.

Rook wants into a back room guarded by three bruisers (Attack +3, Guard 12, 2 Harm each). He wears weighted gloves (light melee).

  1. Round 1, Rook. Free move to engage them, then a Strike with Force: 7+6 +4 = 17 vs 12, margin 5, Impact 2. That's 2 Harm, plus 1 for light melee on a Strike at Impact 2 or better: 3 Harm. Bruisers take Harm the way a named enemy does, so the bonus counts. Every 2 Harm drops one, and the leftover 1 carries to the next.
  2. Pile Through. His Strike dropped a mook, so he gets a free Jab at another in the zone. Light melee has no -2 on a Jab: 3+5 +4 = 12 vs 12, margin 0. A Jab hit is always Impact 1, so 1 Harm. Added to the 1 carried over, the second bruiser drops. Dropping an enemy is his Brawler trigger: +1 Drive (once per round).
  3. Morale. Two of three are down: half the gang. With no named enemy in sight, the last one runs before he can swing.
  4. If he had stayed. His attack: 8+4 +3 = 15 vs 14, Impact 1, 1 Harm. Rook's Toughness 1 (Force +4) cuts it to 0, but a hit always does at least 1: Bruised.

What it shows: one good hit can clear a doorway. Leftover Harm, Pile Through and morale do the work, not three separate rolls.

5Working the leads

Rules: the Patrol pool, the lead table, the police scanner, Upgrade, Plugs and the one no-roll clue.

Night 1 of a case. Two heroes, so the team has a Patrol pool of 2 actions.

  1. Rook leans on the dockworker who quit the week of the fire (a nervous witness, rating -1, defense 11). Action 1 of 2. His police scanner gives Edge on one Patrol roll a Night: 5+3 +1 +2 = 11 vs 11, margin 0, Impact 1. He gets the clue (the man was paid to leave a door unlocked), and the GM offers two costs: tick the Scheme clock, or 1 Stress. Rook takes the Stress.
  2. The Ledger digs through city permits (rating +1, defense 13). Action 2 of 2: 8+6 +4 = 18 vs 13, margin 5, Impact 2. A clean clue: the building was rezoned a month before it burned. Finding a clue is her Detective trigger: +1 Drive.
  3. Upgrade. Ruth spends 2 Drive to raise it to Impact 3 and takes the bonus: Edge on one roll against the villain later. Upgrading works on any roll that hits, leads included.
  4. Plugs. The pool is spent, but Ruth's Flair gets one clue from a contact, no roll. It's the team's one no-roll clue for the Night. Each use ticks her Home or Heat; she ticks Home.
  5. A new lead. The dockworker mentioned a man in a grey suit. That's a new lead, not a clue: the team keeps it for tomorrow's pool.

What it shows: a lead always pays a clue. The roll only sets the price. The pool, not the dice, limits how much the team can learn in one Night.

6Interrogating a lieutenant

Rules: interrogation, Upgrade, stopping the Scheme tick.

Bulwark has the Butcher's lieutenant zip-tied in a stairwell (Poise 14). The capture came out of a lead the team already paid for, so the interrogation costs no Patrol action.

  1. The roll. Dana sits down level with him and talks about his kids: Presence vs Poise. 9+6 +2 = 17 vs 14, margin 3, Impact 1. The clue, and a cost: she picks 1 Stress over a Scheme tick.
  2. Upgrade. Impact 2 would also stop the Scheme clock ticking at the next Dawn, so she spends 2 Drive: Impact 2. He gives up where the next job is, and the Butcher loses a day.
  3. The limit. Only a lieutenant can stop the tick, and only once per case. An accomplice (a fixer, a crooked inspector) gives the clue only.

What it shows: some results are worth buying up. Two Drive here bought the whole team a Night.

7Cracking the case

Rules: the theory, red herrings, personal clues, the Crack roll, whose ground.

Three heroes, so Complexity 8. The team holds 6 clues, enough to try (half the Complexity is 4).

  1. The theory. They agree on who (the landlord's fixer), what (burning out tenants for the rezoning) and where (the Kessler Building, Friday). One clue doesn't fit, a red herring, so it doesn't count. One of the five that fit is personal (the first fire killed Rook's old trainer), so it counts as 2: 6 clues used. Using it ticks Rook's Home clock.
  2. The roll. 2d10 + clues vs 10 + Complexity, so +6 vs 18. Ruth spends 1 Drive for Edge first: 7+4 +6 +2 = 19 vs 18, margin 1, Impact 1. Right, with a snag: they're ready for you.
  3. Whose ground. At Impact 1 the heroes know where, but there's no ambush either way, and the villain starts the showdown with +1 Drive.
  4. If it had missed. The GM says which facet is wrong, and the team picks: Regroup (the Scheme ticks twice; try again with a new clue) or Go in anyway (it ticks once, and they walk into a trap built around the wrong facet).

What it shows: the dice check the theory, not the players' cleverness. More fitting clues are the safest bet: with clues equal to the Complexity, a theory is right 64% of the time.

8Taking the Butcher apart

Rules: Read, using a Weakness, Haymaker, Edge and Advantage together, Toughness, a villain's Trick.

The Butcher of Fifth Street (Guard 12, Wits 13, Harm 10, Toughness 1) and his crew in a meat locker. Two heroes, so he gets one turn a round.

  1. The Ledger reads him (Quick): Focus vs Wits. 7+5 +4 = 16 vs 13, margin 3, Impact 1: one question. "What's his weakness?" The GM answers truthfully: he can't turn down a challenge in front of his crew. The team's next roll against him has Edge, and because she asked about his weakness, a hit with that Edge ignores his Toughness. Learning something true about an enemy: +1 Drive.
  2. Rook plays into it. "Your boys know you're scared of me?" A known Weakness is a promise: it works, no roll. The Butcher waves his men back and comes at Rook alone.
  3. Haymaker (3 Drive): a Strike with Advantage and +1 Harm, plus the Edge from the Read. Three dice, keep two: 8, 7, (3) +4 +2 = 21 vs 12, margin 9, Impact 3. Harm: 3, +1 Haymaker, +1 light melee = 5, and Toughness doesn't apply. Half his boxes in one swing, and Haymaker knocks him into the next zone.
  4. His answer. Taking Impact 2+ is the Heavy's Drive trigger. His Trick, Rampage, lets him Strike right back at whoever hit him, but only if he can reach them. He's a zone away. The knockback took his best move off the table.

What it shows: a villain is beaten by stacking: information, then a Weakness, then the big spend. Edge (+2) and Advantage (the third die) are different things and both apply.

9A chase across the rooftops

Rules: chases, Impact 3 counting double, Drive for Edge, soft moves.

A Trickster lieutenant called Slick (Flow +3, Guard 15) bolts across the roofs. Nightjar follows. Each round she rolls Flow vs his Guard: three hits and she catches him, three Misses and he's gone.

  1. Round 1. 5+3 +4 = 12 vs 15, a Miss (Misses: 1). Soft move, said out loud: "He's heading for the fire escape on the far side. Miss again and he's down on the street."
  2. Round 2. 9+7 +4 = 20 vs 15, margin 5, Impact 2. Hits: 1.
  3. Round 3. The fire escape is close, so she spends 1 Drive for Edge. 10+8 +4 +2 = 24 vs 15, margin 9, Impact 3. In a chase an Impact 3 counts as two hits: 3. She tackles him onto the gravel ten feet from the edge.

What it shows: a chase is a race between two counters, and the GM's soft moves give it a shape. Running away works the same way, rolled against the pursuers' Wits.

10The cops arrive

Rules: Heat 3+, Cops in a fight, grabbed and cuffed, escalation, leaving a fight, Busted.

Heat is at 4, so one group of Cops (Attack +1) arrives on round 3 and another every round after. Rook's police scanner hears them coming a round early.

  1. Round 3, the cops. They go for Patchwork: 9+5 +1 = 15 vs 14, a hit. A cop hit grabs instead of dealing Harm. Patchwork can't take a free move or leave the zone.
  2. Rook's choice. He could flatten them, but the first time a hero attacks a cop, Heat goes up by 1, and from then on the cops deal Harm and their pistols follow the gun rules. He keeps fighting the Butcher's crew instead.
  3. Round 4, the cops. A hit on someone already grabbed cuffs them: 6+7 +1 = 14 vs 14. Patchwork is cuffed and can't act, except a Full Action to slip them (Force or Flow vs 13).
  4. Round 4, Rook. He spends his Full Action next to Patchwork to free him, no roll.
  5. Round 5. Both are in a zone with an exit and not engaged, so each spends a turn to leave. If Patchwork had still been cuffed when the team left, he'd have been arrested: Dawn in a cell, no Dawn picks, and his Home clock ticks for the missed Show Up.

What it shows: cops aren't another gang to beat up. They turn the fight into a getaway, and fighting them makes everything worse.

11A hard Dawn

Rules: the Dawn Scheme tick, Drive at Dawn, the two picks, the Home clock, Dawn moves.

Patchwork ends the Night at 6 Stress, Wounded, with 5 Drive and his Home clock at 3.

  1. The Scheme ticks first. At Dawn every hero takes 1 Stress from it: Patchwork is at 7.
  2. Drive. Above 3 drops to 3, so 5 becomes 3.
  3. Two picks. He needs Sleep It Off (clear 3 Stress, down to 4) and Patch Up (clear Hurt and Wounded). He's still Fraying at 4, but able to fight. He skips Show Up, so his Home clock ticks to 4.
  4. Past half. Someone close was already asking questions. Now the clock is past half, so the GM makes a Dawn move, Someone noticed: his sister sees the cuff marks on his wrists and asks him straight out where he goes at night.
  5. The other choice. Show Up and Sleep It Off would have kept Home at 3 and dropped Stress to 3, but he'd start the next Night Wounded, at Disadvantage on Force and Flow.

What it shows: Dawn never lets you fix everything. Every pick you skip is a cost you chose, and the Home clock is where the mask's price builds up.

12Rook breaks

Rules: the hook, Presence attacks, Strain, Breaking, Grit your teeth, After Breaking.

Rook (Spite) is at 6 Stress, so he's Fraying. Spite's hook: he can't back down from an insult or a challenge.

  1. The hook. The Butcher's lieutenant laughs at him from across a burning loading bay. The GM pushes the hook: "You're going over there, aren't you?" Rook goes along with it: +1 Drive. He charges into the Hazard zone.
  2. The free Presence attack. The Butcher's threat, once per round, on top of his turn: 9+4 +2 = 15 vs 13, margin 2, Impact 1. 1 Stress: 7.
  3. Strain. Rook wants one more Quick Action and takes 1 Stress for it: 8. He's Breaking. Spite's fallout takes over for the rest of the scene: he attacks whoever is closest, friend or foe. His Fraying perk is off, and he can't grit his teeth to ignore Wounded at 8 Stress.
  4. The fallout. The closest person is Bulwark, who stepped in to cover him. Rook swings at her: 6+3 +4 = 13 vs 13, margin 0, Impact 1. 1 Harm, and her Toughness can't take a hit below 1. Not an enemy's attack, so it doesn't trigger her Protector Drive.
  5. After the scene. Rook drops to 4 Stress, gains 2 Drive, and his Home clock ticks once. It followed him home.

What it shows: Breaking comes from many small choices: taking the hook, taking the Strain. Each one paid off on its own. The table sees it coming, and the Drive from Breaking is what lets the hero come back from it.
