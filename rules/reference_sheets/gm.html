Making a move

A move is something you add to the scene that the heroes now have to deal with. Make one when:

  • a roll Misses;
  • a clock hits a beat or fills;
  • the players hand you an opening: they split up, ignore a threat you named, or look at you to see what happens.

Three habits

  • Start from the fiction. Pick the move the scene is already pointing at.
  • Say the threat first. A soft move tells them what's about to happen and leaves them a turn to stop it.
  • Then follow through. If they don't stop it, it happens. That's a hard move, and they were warned.

Soft move: makes things worse. No Harm, no Stress, no clock tick.

Hard move: costs something real, and the hero it lands on also takes 1 Stress.

Hard moves follow the hero's Trait when they can. A Fraying hero's hook is always a fair soft move.

Costs (Impact 1)

When a roll at Impact 1 comes with a cost, offer two and let the player pick. Attacks never carry a cost. At 8 Stress, leave Stress off and offer two others.

  • 1 Harm.
  • 1 Stress.
  • A clock ticks (the fight's clock, the Scheme, Heat).
  • You lose your spot: engaged, in the open, or cut off from the team.
  • You lose a thing: a weapon skitters away, a tool breaks, a charge is used up.
  • One of your connections gets pulled in.

Moves in a fight

A Miss earns a soft move, because the enemy still gets its own turn. A hard move needs a reason:

  • the hero was warned (you said out loud what would happen);
  • the roll had Disadvantage;
  • it's that hero's second Miss in a row.

A Miss in a Hazard zone already costs 1 Harm; that alone isn't a reason.

Soft

  • Reposition. An enemy takes Cover, climbs to High Ground, or cuts off an exit.
  • Close in. An enemy engages a hero who wanted to stay clear, or pins one in a Cramped zone.
  • Backup. A mook group arrives next round. Say where from.
  • Move the stake. The hostage is dragged a zone away, the evidence is picked up, the van starts.
  • Change the ground. The lights go out, a fire spreads, a crowd pours in. A zone gains a tag.
  • Name the next hit. Say who the villain is going for and how. If nobody stops it, that attack has Edge.

Hard

  • Hurt. An enemy gets a free Jab at the hero (armor and Toughness apply).
  • Tick. A clock in play ticks.
  • Strip. The hero is disarmed, or one piece of gear is smashed until Dawn.
  • Separate. A door slams, a floor gives way, smoke rolls in. A Quick Action to get back to the team.
  • Take. The villain gets something they came for: a step of their plan, a hostage, an exit.
  • Finish them. An enemy goes for a Downed hero. The hardest move in the game: only a killer does it, and you always say it out loud first. It leaves the hero Dying; whether they die is still their player's call.

Moves on Patrol

A missed lead uses its own cost table. Use these for a Miss on any other Patrol roll, and when a Scheme beat lands.

Soft

  • Clam up. A witness goes quiet, or a contact wants a favor first.
  • Eyes on you. A tail, a camera, a parked car with the engine running.
  • Beaten to it. The person has moved on, the place is picked over.
  • Two ways. The clue fits two suspects; it takes one more fact to choose.
  • A knock at the door. Someone from a hero's connections shows up at the worst time.
  • Show the villain's hand. They see the next Scheme beat coming and can spend a Patrol action to get in its way.

Hard

  • Tick. The Scheme clock ticks.
  • Heat. +1 Heat: a description, a phone video, a cop who remembers a face.
  • Ambush. A fight starts on their ground.
  • Burn a source. A contact, lead or tool is gone for the rest of the case.
  • Lean on a connection. Someone with leverage makes a call. Tick that hero's Home.
  • Make it personal. The trouble lands on someone the hero protects. They're where the next beat happens.

Moves at Dawn

Dawn has no rolls, so no Misses. Use these when a hero skips Show Up, when a Home clock passes half, and when Heat passes half.

  • Someone noticed. A bruise, a missed shift, a lie that didn't hold. A connection asks a straight question.
  • The bill comes due. Whoever has leverage over the hero wants something by tomorrow night.
  • The city talks. A headline, a rumor, a sketch on the news. Say what people now believe about the heroes.
  • Empty chair. Something happened in the hero's civilian life while they were out, and they weren't there.

Building a villain fast

  • Stats total 7 or 8 for a case villain, 4 to 7 for a lieutenant. Defenses as heroes (12 + stat).
  • Role: Heavy, Schemer, Nemesis, Trickster or Insidious (sets how they earn Drive).
  • Trait: one line for Fraying (gives the heroes an opening) and one for Breaking (reckless). A hero Trait works, or a villain one: Greed, Desperation, Narcissism, Lust, Viciousness, Paranoia, Envy, Contempt, Zealotry, Control.
  • Presence attack (free, once per round): +2 or +3 vs Poise, 1 Stress per Impact, with a line they say.
  • Trick: one special move for 1 or 2 Drive.
  • Weakness: something the heroes can find and play into.
  • A way past Toughness.

Setting a difficulty

The defense is 12 + a stat: the target's, or an obstacle's rating.

RatingDefExamples
-111A locked door, a chain-link fence, a nervous witness
+113A police database, a three-story drop, a crowd turning ugly
+315A corporate server, a car going 60, a hardened mob boss

No real opposition and failure isn't interesting? Don't roll. A defense of 15+ means a +3 hero misses about half the time: one such enemy per fight at most, with a way to get Edge.

Running enemies

Enemies roll 2d10 + their stat vs the hero's defense. Sides alternate turns; heroes go first unless ambushed. Tick fight clocks at the end of each round.

HarmStress (Fray / Break)
Mook1None

Lieutenant54 (2 / 4)
Case villain10 (8 smaller); +2 alone6 (3 / 6)

Scaling to the party

  • Villain turns: one per round per two heroes, rounded up. Fighting alone (no mooks or lieutenants): one per hero, and +2 Harm.
  • Lieutenant: one turn and one free Presence attack per round, always.
  • Several named enemies: each villain by the rule above, each lieutenant one. Nobody counts as alone.
  • Free Presence attack: every named villain, once per round, on top of their turn. Against a solo hero, every other round, starting round 1.
  • Gang size: no named villain, one group of 3 per hero. With one, one group per two heroes (rounded down, min 1). Harder: make a group bruisers. Don't add groups on top of a villain.

Mooks

  • Any hit takes one out. A real Impact 3 drops a second in the zone.
  • Groups of up to 3, one attack per group per turn, Edge if all 3 stand.
  • Mook attacks deal at most 2 Harm, applied last.
  • Bruisers: every 2 Harm drops one, leftover carries over. Weapon bonuses count. No Toughness.
  • Morale: a gang runs when half are down, unless a named enemy is in sight. Cornered, they give up. Fanatics, animals, swarms, the mind-controlled and cops never run.

Named enemies

  • Lose at most one turn per fight. After that, a lost turn is Disadvantage on their next roll (not counting the free Presence attack).
  • Breaking villain: stays in, Breaking line runs the scene, attacks against them have Edge. If they flee, heroes get one round.
  • Drive: start at 2, earn from the role trigger once per round, same prices as heroes.
  • Weakness: when heroes play into a known one, it works, no roll. If a roll comes of it, the first each scene has Edge.
  • Give every villain a way past Toughness: fire, electricity, gas, a hazard, or Presence.

The fight's goal

Every Confrontation has an Objective of 2 to 4 steps against the fight's clock. A step is a Full Action and a roll vs -1, +1 or +3, at a named place. Write what each step changes right away (the clock stops, a tag changes, a gang runs). Finishing the Objective wins the fight.

Defeat: every hero Down or fled. The villain gets what they came for, Scheme ticks twice, each hero ticks Home or Heat. Nobody dies from defeat alone.

Case prep

The most a case needs, not the least. Often just the villain; improvise the rest from the heroes' neighborhoods and connections.

  • Three facets: who's behind it, what they want, where they'll strike.
  • Cast: 4 to 6 suspects or factions, each with a motive and a secret.
  • Clues: Complexity + 4 concrete facts, each fitting more than one suspect. 1 or 2 personal clues.
  • 3 to 5 leads to go lean on.
  • Complexity: 4 for a one-Night job; else 2 + twice the heroes (6 for one or two, 8 for three, 10 for four). Arc finale +2.
  • Scheme clock of 6, with beats at 1, 3 and 5.

Patrol pool: one action per hero (min 2) per Night; a lead costs one and buys everything in that thread. About one clue per hero per Night.

Interrogation: Presence vs a captured named enemy's Poise. Impact 2+ from a lieutenant also stops the next Dawn Scheme tick (once per case).

Cracking and the trap

Roll 2d10 + clues used vs 10 + Complexity. With clues = Complexity, 64% right; +2 clues, 79%.

Go in anyway on a Miss and it's a trap: the villain's ground, their ambush, a lieutenant or gang instead of the boss.

  • Wrong Who: a patsy, and a clean-up crew. Objective: keep the patsy alive.
  • Wrong What: a live job. +1 Heat, cops on round 2.
  • Wrong Where: a decoy. One zone is a Hazard; a clock of 4 counts down.

Win it: 1 clue and the true answer to the wrong facet.

Killing: your call whether it adds Heat. A vanished criminal may draw none.

Crackdown (pick one): a task force (cops every fight on round 2, led by the Commander), a smear (Presence vs civilians and cops at Disadvantage next case), or a description (every hero ticks Home).

Cops in a fight (Heat 3+): a group arrives on round 3 and another each round after. A cop hit grabs instead of hurting; a hit on someone grabbed or Down cuffs them. The first time a hero attacks a cop, +1 Heat, and the cops deal Harm from then on. Still cuffed when the team leaves: arrested, no Dawn picks, back by the next Night.

Hidden: an enemy searches with a Quick Action, Focus vs 12 + the hero's Flow (mooks use their attack bonus).

Chases: three hits catch them (Impact 3 counts as two), three Misses lose them.

Between cases: Harm clears, Stress above 3 drops to 3, Drive resets, Heat -2, Home stays, one Advancement pick each.
